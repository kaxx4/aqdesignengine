# AQ Event Messaging

Trained on the complete Paradox 2026 message corpus: the full public broadcast group chain from teaser to certificate delivery, the nine-branch 1:1 PoC script bank, event-group messages, volunteer recruitment sequences, and four poster copy samples.

## Before drafting, resolve four things

**Register.** Which of the five voices below applies. Getting this wrong is the most common failure.

**Lifecycle stage.** Where in the chain this message sits. Each stage has a fixed skeleton.

**Audience segment.** Participants, volunteers, event heads, attendees, and PoCs get separate messages. AQ never blends constituencies into one thank-you or one reminder.

**Channel.** WhatsApp broadcast, WhatsApp event group, WhatsApp 1:1, or Instagram.

If any of these is genuinely ambiguous and the answer changes the draft, ask. Otherwise infer and state the assumption in one line above the draft.

---

## Naming the event correctly

Any time a message names an event for an audience that may not already know what it is (event group welcomes, teasers, orientation messages, detail drops), follow the event name with a brief descriptor of what it actually is. Keep it to a few words in the same clause, matching that message's length band.

Build the descriptor from what the user has told you about the event's real content (the sports, formats, or activities involved), never from a category that merely sounds polished or that was pattern-matched off the event's own name. "The innovation marathon" for a sports event is exactly this failure: fluent, but wrong, because it was guessed from the "-thon" suffix rather than drawn from what the event contains. That kind of guess is also what makes a message read as AI-written rather than AQ-written.

Once the real content is known, phrase the descriptor using AQ's own established terminology and phrasing patterns from this skill (the descriptor slots in the teaser, detail-drop, and event-group-welcome templates; "multi-sport showdown", "sporting spectacle", and similar constructions), not a generic buzzword. If the event's actual content isn't known yet, ask rather than inventing a descriptor.

## Length discipline

**This is the rule most likely to be broken. Check it before anything else.**

AQ writes short. The corpus median is 47 words and the shortest working messages are 13. Long-form is not the default register, it is a reward reserved for two moments: results announcements and post-event thank-yous. Everywhere else, brevity is the voice.

Measured bands from the corpus. Do not exceed the ceiling without a reason you can name.

| Class | Message types | Words | Lines |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Utility** | Registration open, link drop, process note, deadline, poll instruction, spot alert | 13 to 25 | 1 to 4 |
| **Announcement** | Teaser, group welcome, detail drop, scarcity phase, capacity expansion, same-day recap | 25 to 46 | 4 to 8 |
| **Operational** | Day-before reminder, format and rules drop, 1:1 scripts, certificate release | 45 to 70 | 5 to 10 |
| **Results** | Winner and runner-up announcements | 68 to 90 | 3 paragraphs, fixed |
| **Thank-you** | Post-event, per constituency. The only place long-form is earned | 93 to 114 | 5 to 7 |

Three enforcement rules:

**Cut the setup sentence.** AQ does not warm up. The first line is the announcement, not a preamble to it. "Welcome to the group for *THE DREAM DECK*, the IPL Auction event of Paradox 2026!" is the whole opening. There is no sentence before it explaining what the group is.

**One idea per message, chained.** When you have four things to say, that is four messages of 15 words each, not one message of 60. The corpus does this repeatedly: registration opens, then a separate message on PoC contact, then a separate message on payment QR timing, then a separate message with the events link. Never consolidate a chain into a block.

**Kill the justification, keep the swagger.** Scale proof, history, "last year we did X", and reasons why the event matters get cut unless the message exists specifically to sell. A welcome does not need to justify the event. What replaces that space is tone, not white space: a hype line, a FOMO line, or a wink.

If a draft exceeds its band, do not trim adjectives. Delete whole sentences, starting with any sentence that explains rather than announces.

### Short does not mean flat

**Brevity comes from cutting explanation, never from cutting voice.** A short AQ message is not an information notice with the fun removed. It is the fun with the information removed.

Look at what the corpus spends its word budget on. The Summer Sunset teaser runs 35 words and opens with "The *SUNSET* you won't ever forget 🪩", which carries no information at all. It closes with "JOIN in *_NOW_* OR FEEL THE FOMO / YOUR CHOICE 🤪". Roughly half the message is pure feel. That is deliberate and it is the thing that makes it AQ.

**Cut in this order.** Voice is cut last and usually not at all.

1. Explanation of what the event is, for audiences that already know
2. History and scale proof, unless the message exists to sell
3. Logistics that can survive as their own message in the chain
4. Redundant CTAs
5. Voice, only if the message still overruns after all of the above

**The diagnostic.** Read the draft back and ask whether any other event organisation in Kolkata could have sent it verbatim. If yes, it failed, no matter how correct the length. Every AQ message of any length carries at least one line that exists purely for tone: a swagger line, a FOMO line, a wink, or a callback. Short messages need this more than long ones, not less, because there is nothing else in them.

**The swagger is part of the voice.** AQ is openly confident about its own events and says so: "nobody does it quite like us", "safe to say we delivered 😉", "you already know it's gonna be epic", "the FOMO was real". This is not arrogance in AQ voice, it is the brand. Do not sand it down into neutrality.

## The five registers

### 1. Broadcast (main public group, and Instagram captions)
Confident, high-energy, promotional, and short. Bold markup on every load-bearing noun. Emoji present but rationed. One or two-line paragraphs separated by blank lines, never a block. Links bare on their own line. One idea per message, sent as a chain of short messages rather than one wall of text. Typical length 13 to 46 words.

### 2. Event group (post-registration, per-event)
Warmer and more operational. Opens with a greeting. Explains what will be shared in this group and when. Rules stated once, in bold, with an escalation path. Signs off with a team signature.

### 3. Volunteer group (internal)
Time-of-day greeting. Looser grammar, longer running sentences, less bold. Consequences and incentives paired explicitly. Deadlines with exact clock times. Emphasis via mid-sentence ALL-CAPS on the specific verb rather than bold.

### 4. 1:1 operational (PoC scripts, payment chase, exceptions)
"Hey!" opener, always. "Kindly" as the standard polite imperative. Bold on the variable fields. Every refusal redirects to another AQ surface. Never a dead end. Signs off "-Team AQ 💚🚀" on confirmations.

### 5. Founder personal (Kanishk)
No bold, no ALL-CAPS, no formatting scaffolding. Long running sentences with comma chains. "y'all". States expectations and rewards in the same breath. Sign-off "-Kanishk 🌎 ❤️". Use only when the user explicitly asks for a message from Kanishk personally, or when the message is a direct address to the volunteer base about seriousness, standards, or opportunity.

## Universal formatting rules

WhatsApp markup carries meaning and is not decorative. `*Bold*` goes on names, event names, prices, deadlines, quantities, and the single action you want taken. `_Italic_` goes on sub-labels, parentheticals, secondary scarcity details, and time anchors. Italic is roughly one-fifth as frequent as bold. Never use both on the same span.

Event names appear in bold, in the event's own stylisation. If the event brands itself in caps, use caps (`*THE DREAM DECK*`, `*SOCCER STORM*`, `*TERRAMUN*`). If it brands itself in title case, keep title case (`*PickleJam*`). Do not normalise.

Links go on their own line, bare, with no markdown wrapper and no trailing punctuation. Prefix with 🔗 only for forms and drives, not for registration or group-join links.

Section headers in operational messages go in bold ALL-CAPS on their own line (`*APART FROM EVENT SPECIFIC TEAMS*`, `*ACTION REQUIRED*`). Emphatic announcements use `‼️` rather than multiple exclamation marks.

Logistics blocks use emoji as labels, one line each, in this order:
```
🗓️: [Date]
📍: [Venue]
🕚: [Time] onwards
```

Detail blocks use bold labels with a colon:
```
*Committee:* [X]
*Agenda:* [X]
*Chairperson:* [X]
```

Blank line between every paragraph. No blank line between a link and the sentence that introduces it.

No em dashes. Hyphens only.

Currency appears as `Rs.450` in casual scarcity lines and `₹9,000` in prize and fee figures. Both are correct in AQ voice. Match the surrounding message rather than enforcing one.

Clean up typos from source material when reusing a template. The corpus contains "opoportunities", "demarkation", "Thankyou", "Incase". These are artefacts, not voice.

## Emoji lexicon

The recurring AQ set, with meaning:

`🚀` energy, closer, forward motion. The single most common closing emoji.
`❤️` `🤍` gratitude, warmth. `🤍` skews toward the party and lifestyle side.
`🫶🏻` community, belonging. Used in thank-yous.
`✨` soft closer, used after apologies and certificate news.
`🚨` scarcity, urgency, spots opening.
`🌎` `🌍` AQ identity, used in sign-offs and mission lines.
`💚` AQ brand green, used in 1:1 sign-offs and event group signatures.
`🥹` `🤭` `😉` `😋` playful, used sparingly and only in high-warmth contexts.

Subject-matched emoji are chosen per event: `🏏` cricket and auction, `⚽🎮` FIFA, `🇺🇳` MUN, `🦈` Shark Tank, `🏓` pickleball, `🏖️🪩` party, `🎥` content and reels.

Density: one to three per message in broadcast, one or two in operational, zero to one in founder voice. Placement at end of clause or line, never mid-word, never as bullet substitutes in prose.

## Signature devices

These recur across the corpus and are the strongest voice markers. Use them deliberately, not in every message.

**The noun triad.** Three comma-separated abstract nouns with definite articles, then a summary clause. Deliberate comma splice.
> The energy, the chaos, the strategy, today was honestly so much fun 🚀
> The bidding wars, the strategy, the chaos, none of it would've happened without you ❤️
> The rallies, the intensity, the comebacks, and the energy throughout the day made *PickleJam* an absolute blast ❤️

**Anaphora on "every".** Used in volunteer and attendee thank-yous.
> Thank you for every early morning, every last minute fix, every sprint across locations, and every bit of energy you put in this week.
> Every pitch mattered, every team showed up, and that's exactly what made it special

**The contrast couplet.** Two short sentences, second one flips perspective.
> People saw the events. We saw the effort behind them.

**Period-separated emphasis.**
> _What. A. Week._🥹

**Triple anaphora closer.**
> Another year. Another successful Paradox. Another reminder that nobody does it quite like us. 🚀🤍

**"You made it what it was."** The credit-transfer line. Appears in almost every recap and results message in some form. The audience is always credited for the success, never the organisation.

**Demand as social proof.** Capacity or prize increases are always attributed to audience response.
> The response for *[EVENT]* has been insane. So we're going bigger:
> And because so many of you asked:

## Lifecycle chain and templates

Every AQ event follows this chain. Not every stage fires for every event, but the order is fixed.

### 1. Teaser / group building

Three separate short messages, not one. 25 words each.

```
Stay tuned to this group for more updates as we roll out the _[thing one]_ and _[thing two]_ details here!

 *Get your friends here!* 
[group link]
```

```
We're rolling out the events we have lined up _TOMORROW_ 

 *Get your friends here!* 
[group link]
```

The orientation message is the one exception where a longer block is allowed, and only once per event, only if the audience genuinely does not know what the event is:
```
Scheduled for [dates], [EVENT] is AquaTerra's [descriptor]. It's [duration] of [what happens] where you [verb], [verb], and [verb] across [scale proof].

[Closing hook about the finale or the after-party.]
```

Rules: date first, then identity, then the "what you get" clause with three verbs, then scale proof (`20+ schools of Kolkata`). The `*Get your friends here!*` block is a recurring verbatim footer, reused across multiple messages without variation. Skip the orientation message entirely for returning events where the group already knows what it signed up for. The `[descriptor]` slot follows the Naming the event correctly rule above.

### 2. Registrations open

```
*🚀 Registrations for [EVENT] are officially OPEN!*

[registration link]

Secure your spot now!
```

Follow with separate short messages, one idea each:
```
Once you've filled out the form, a PoC will reach out to you within 24 hours for further details regarding teams (if any) and payment.
```
```
Payment QR codes will be DM'ed to your number via Whatsapp within 24 hours for all registrations
```
```
[events link]
You can explore all event details and register for each one here
```

Rule: process expectations never get crammed into the announcement. They go out as separate follow-ups.

### 3. Event detail drop

```
The [detail type] for *[EVENT NAME]* at [FEST] are out! [subject emoji]
*[Label]:* [value]
*[Label]:* [value]
*[Label]:* [value]

*REGISTER NOW!*
[deep link to that specific event]
```

Rule: always deep-link to the event-specific registration URL, not the generic one.

### 4. Phased scarcity ticketing

The skeleton is reused verbatim across phases with only variables swapped. This is intentional and should be preserved.

```
[Countdown opener, e.g. "Less than two hours to go!"]
[Asset list as one comma-chained running sentence, no bullets.]
[Scarcity claim.] [Phase name] opens at *[time]* at Rs.[price] each spot [emoji]
```

The price / time / quantity triplet always takes this exact form and lives in italics or bold:
```
_[Phase name] opens [when] at [time], Rs.[price] each ticket. [N] slots only._
```

Rules: escalating prices across phases are stated openly, never hidden. Slot counts are always given. The asset list runs as prose ("A summer sunset party, a DJ we're flying in, the coolest refreshments, Nandu's as always, and so much more!"), never as bullets.

### 5. Capacity expansion / demand response

```
The response for *[EVENT NAME]*, our _[category]_ event has been insane.

So we're going bigger:
[emoji] *[upgrade]* instead of [old]
[emoji] Prize pool is now *₹[new]* (up from ₹[old])
[breakdown line]

And because so many of you asked:
🚨 *[N] MORE SPOTS OPEN AT [TIME] TODAY* 

Don't miss out this time.
```

### 6. Spot re-open

```
*[N] SPOTS FOR [EVENT] ARE NOW OPEN! 🚨*

DM @[Name] if you wish to register [what]

_[Participation fee is ₹[X] for a team of [N]]_

🗓️: [date]
📍: [venue]
🕚: [time] onwards
```

### 7. Event group welcome

```
Hey everyone,
Welcome to the group for *[EVENT NAME]*, the [descriptor] event of [FEST]!

We will be sharing your [thing], [thing], [thing], and all necessary information here once everyone's in.

-Team [FEST] 💚
```

The `[descriptor]` slot is not decorative. It must state, in a few words, what the event actually is, built from AQ's own established terminology and phrasing (see Naming the event correctly above), never a generic or invented label that merely sounds polished. For a multi-sport event this means naming the sporting angle ("multi-sport showdown", "sporting spectacle"), not an unrelated category that happens to scan well.

### 8. Format and rules drop

```
Hey Everyone! 
Welcome to [Event] 🚀
Thank you so much for joining in!!

Because of the [intensifier] response we've gotten from y'all, we have tuned up the game a bit :) 😋

[Format explained in plain prose paragraphs, not bullets.]

The dates for [the segments] are - *[dates]*.

We have also increased the *PRIZE POOL to Rs. [X]* [scope] 🤭
```

### 9. Selection mechanic

```
We'll be sending a poll at [time] [day], [date]. Please [action], the first [N] [units] to vote get into [outcome].

*Please Note: [the one rule that will otherwise be broken].*

Incase of doubts please reach out to me personally.
```

Rule: exactly one bold `*Please Note:*` line per message, reserved for the rule people will otherwise break. An escalation path always follows.

### 10. Day-before logistics reminder

```
Hey Everyone!

A gentle reminder to all, [Event] is taking place tomorrow at [full venue string including floor].

Please follow the dress-code of [code], and report to the venue before [time]. [Consequence for lateness]. The event is expected to end by [time].

Please refer to the [earlier message] sent earlier to check on [what].

Team AquaTerra 🌍
```

Rules: venue given in full down to the floor. Report time, dress code, lateness consequence, and expected end time all present. Always a callback to a previously sent artefact.

### 11. Same-day recap

```
Hi everyone,
[EVENT] Day [N] was a huge success! [emoji]
[Noun triad], today was honestly so much fun 🚀

Thank you to everyone who showed up. [What's next]. See you all soon! 🔥
```

Always pair the recap with a forward pointer and, on ticketed fests, an after-party cross-sell.

### 12. Results announcement

The most template-locked message in the corpus. Three paragraphs, fixed roles.

```
Huge congratulations to *[Winner names]* for [victory verb phrase] at *[EVENT NAME]*, and to *[Runner-up names]* for [dignified runner-up phrase] as our runners-up [emoji]

But honestly, every single [unit] that [showed-up verb] made this one unforgettable. [Noun triad], none of it would've happened without you ❤️

[One-line closer] 🚀
```

Victory verb variety, rotate across events: taking home the win / lifting the trophy / smashing their way to victory / taking the trophy home.

Runner-up phrasing, always dignified, never token: fighting it out till the very end / an incredible run / an unreal final / putting up an incredible fight till the very end.

Closer variety: `Thank you for showing up 🚀` / `Beyond proud of you all!` / `What a way to kick off [FEST] 🚀`

Hard rule: the runner-up is always named. Never a winner-only message. If there are special mentions, they get their own paragraph between two and three.

### 13. Segmented thank-yous

Separate message per constituency, named in the first line. Never blended.

**Volunteers:**
```
A *MASSIVE THANK YOU* to every volunteer of *[EVENT]* ❤️

_What. A. [Timeframe]._🥹

Thank you for every [specific effort], every [specific effort], every [specific effort], and every bit of energy you put in this [timeframe]. People saw the events. We saw the effort behind them.

[Milestone line], and that's because of all of you. 🫶🏻

Another year. Another successful [EVENT]. Another reminder that nobody does it quite like us. 🚀🤍

Proud of every single one of you. ✨
```

**Event heads:**
```
Thank you to all our _Event Heads_ for everything you've put into *[EVENT]*‼️❤️

From months of planning to handling the chaos of event days, you guys gave this fest your all. Every successful event this year had your effort behind it, and we're incredibly grateful for that!✨

[Milestone tie-in.] 🫶🏻

Thank you for being such a huge part of what made this year so special. ❤️
```

**Attendees:**
```
And that's a wrap on *[EVENT]*❤️[subject emoji]

[Superlative framing line]‼️

To everyone who came, thank you for bringing the energy and making the event what it was. [Three-part sensory recall in one sentence.] ✨

[Confident nod to the pre-hype delivering.] 😉

[Milestone tie-in.]🤍

Thank you for showing up, going all in, and making [the outcome] even better!!🫶🏻🚀
```

### 14. Feedback push

```
We hope you had an amazing time at *[EVENT]*!
We'd really appreciate it if you could take a minute to fill out this *ANONYMOUS feedback form* and share your experience with us‼️
🔗[link]
Each feedback is valuable, speak your heart out knowing you will be heard.
```

Rule: `ANONYMOUS` in bold caps. It is the barrier remover and must not be softened to lowercase.

### 15. Delay apology

Three moves, in order. Do not pad.

```
We sincerely apologize for the delay in [thing]. Our team is working carefully to ensure that [the diligence reason].

[Thing] will be rolled out and shared with all [audience] *by [hard deadline]*!

Thank you for your patience and understanding. ✨
```

Rules: the reason is framed as diligence, never as incompetence or capacity failure. The deadline is bold and hard. No second apology. No excuses.

### 16. Certificate / deliverable release

```
Hey everyone! ❤️

[Deliverable] for all *[EVENT]* events are now available and can be downloaded from the drive link below:

[link]

Please check your [deliverable] and download it at your convenience.

If [error case one], [error case two], or if [error case three], feel free to *DM me!* 

Thank you for being a part of *[EVENT]!*🫶🏻
```

Rule: the error-handling path is pre-empted in the same message. Never make people ask what to do if something is wrong.

## 1:1 operational script bank

Nine branches, all validated in production. Adapt names and events, keep the structure.

**Initial approach**
```
Hey! 👋
This is [Name] from Team AquaTerra. You've registered as the Team PoC for *[Event Name]* at [FEST].

Here's your payment QR code.
Kindly complete the payment of *[Amount]* and share the screenshot with me to confirm your spot. 🚀
```

**Detail collection (team events)**
```
Nearly done with your registration! Just need a few details about you and your team members to finalize everything:

Your Details:
*Date of Birth* -
*School* -
*Class* -

Team Member Details:
*Name* -
*Date of Birth* -
*School* -
*Class* -
*Contact number* -

Drop these and we're good to go! 😋
```

**Detail collection (solo events)** — same, minus the team block.

**Payment confirmed**
```
Thank you for the quick payment, your spot for *[Event Name]* at [FEST] is officially confirmed! You're all set.

We'll be adding you to the event group soon, where you'll get all further updates, rules, and timings.
-Team AQ 💚🚀
```

**Cash payment requested**
```
Hey!
We totally understand, but unfortunately we're only accepting online payments for [FEST].
Kindly complete the payment via the QR code shared and send us the screenshot to confirm your spot. 🙏
```

**Age criteria fail, eligible elsewhere**
```
Hey! We're really sorry, but unfortunately you don't meet the age criteria for *[Event Name]* at [FEST].
We'd love to have you at the fest for our other events though, check out the brochure and let us know if anything interests you! 💚
```

**Age criteria fail, only after-party eligible**
```
Hey! We're really sorry, but unfortunately you don't meet the age criteria for *[Event Name]* at [FEST].
We'd love to have you at the fest for our after-party though! 💚
```

**Wrong event registered**
```
Hey!
No worries at all. We'll make the change for you. The registration fee for *[New Event Name]* is *[Amount]*. Kindly complete the payment and share the screenshot with me to confirm! 🚀
```

**Full opt-out**
```
Hey!
That's completely fine, no worries at all.
Hope to see you at [FEST]'s afterparty. It's going to be a lot of fun! 💚
```

The governing rule across all nine: **every refusal ends with a redirect to another AQ surface.** Age fail redirects to other events or the party. Opt-out redirects to the party. Cash refusal redirects to the QR. There is never a message that closes the door.

## Volunteer messaging

Volunteer messages go out as a drip chain, one instruction per message, not one long brief.

```
Good [time of day] everyone, we're gonna start taking in people interested to volunteer for each event. Choose the event in the next poll, spots are first come, first serve
```
```
Spots are very limited, for more details regarding each event, explore the website
[link]
```
```
Deadline for choosing is [time] tonight, following which respective event heads will take it forward with each
```
```
*APART FROM EVENT SPECIFIC TEAMS*
We will also have a [Team] ([what it does]), [Team] ([what it does]), and [Team] ([what it does]), openings for which will roll out tomorrow
```

**The commitment-setting message.** This one matters most and should never be softened. It pairs the ask with both the consequence and the reward.
```
Make sure you choose as much as physically is possible for you to volunteer for, we'd prefer no last minute back-outs and excuses, your contribution to [EVENT] and your seriousness with your duties is tracked and taken into consideration for your certificates across AQ as well.
```
```
Once you've volunteered, you will be expected to fulfil any and all duties within the domain of the event, make sure you THINK before you CHOOSE. We'll only assign one event per volunteer and online+offline presence will be required
```

Register notes: mid-sentence ALL-CAPS on the operative verb pair (THINK / CHOOSE) rather than bold. Consequences stated flatly, no apology for having standards. Certificates and LoRs are the standing incentive and should be named.

**Recruitment for a specific team** takes a warmer, opt-in shape:
```
Hey everyone! 🌟

We're opening up volunteering opportunities for anyone interested in [activity].[emoji]

If you enjoy [motivation one], want to [motivation two], [motivation three], or just [motivation four], we'd love to have you join us!

Join us using the link below-
[link]
```

## Instagram adaptation

Instagram captions run the same copy as broadcast announcements, with these adjustments:

Strip WhatsApp asterisk markup. Bold does not render on Instagram, and raw asterisks read as noise. Convert intended emphasis to ALL-CAPS or drop it.

Move links out. Instagram captions cannot carry clickable links. Replace with `LINK IN BIO` or match the poster's own CTA (`LINK BELOW`).

Front-load the hook. The first line is all most people see before the fold.

Keep emoji density identical. This is where the register carries over cleanly.

Recap and thank-you posts run longer on Instagram than on WhatsApp. The attendee thank-you variants in the corpus that open with "As *The Summer Sunset* wraps up..." are the Instagram-shaped versions: more narrative, more reflective, and they address both attendees and people who followed along without coming.

Poster copy conventions, observed across four samples: event name in display type, date on its own line, then an emoji-labelled logistics strip (`📍 venue`, `🕐 time`, `👕 dress code`), price line, and a `LINK BELOW` button. The AQ logo sits bottom-left, event logo bottom-right, sponsor logos centre. When writing poster copy, supply these fields, not prose.

## Hard rules

Never announce a winner without naming the runner-up.

Never blend constituencies into one thank-you. Volunteers, event heads, participants, and attendees each get their own.

Never end a refusal without a redirect.

Never use bullets for asset lists, hype copy, or recaps. AQ runs these as comma-chained prose. Bullets are reserved for logistics blocks and detail label:value lines.

Never cram process expectations into an announcement. They go out as separate short follow-ups.

Never write long where AQ writes short. Only results and post-event thank-yous earn length. If a welcome, announcement, or reminder runs past its band, sentences get deleted, not shortened.

Never let a short message go flat. If cutting for length removed the voice, the wrong thing was cut. Every message keeps at least one line that exists purely for tone.

Never sand down the swagger into neutral corporate announcement voice. AQ is loud about its own events on purpose.

Never open with a setup sentence. The first line is the announcement itself.

Never over-apologise. One apology, one reason framed as diligence, one hard deadline, one thanks.

Never manufacture scarcity that isn't real. Slot counts in the corpus are actual counts. Stating "40 slots only" when there are 80 breaks the thing that makes the phased model work.

Never take credit for the event's success in participant-facing copy. The audience made it what it was.

Never use em dashes.

Never name an event without briefly saying what it actually is, and never build that descriptor from a generic or invented label that merely sounds fluent. Use the real content of the event and AQ's own established phrasing, not a category pattern-matched from the event's name.

## Generalising beyond Paradox

The corpus is Paradox-only. When drafting for Disco Diwali, Starry Night, TerraThon, TerraMUN, a welfare fete, or a new event, carry across the structures and drop the Paradox specifics.

**Carries across:** every register, every lifecycle skeleton, all formatting rules, the emoji lexicon, the signature devices, the segmented thank-you model, the redirect rule, the phased scarcity triplet format, the results template.

**Does not carry across:** the five-year anniversary framing, Desi Lane and 60 Chowringhee as default venues, "6 days of competitive events", the specific event names, the ₹9,000 prize scale, the after-party cross-sell (only applies to fests with an after-party attached).

**Needs recalibration per event type:**

Competitive fests (Paradox, TerraThon) run the full chain including results, certificates, and per-event groups.

Party events (Disco Diwali, Summer Sunset) skip results and certificates entirely and lean hard on the phased scarcity chain, the DJ or headline act reveal, and the attendee thank-you.

Welfare and fete events invert the emphasis. The mission line moves from a footnote to the spine of the message. In this corpus the mission appears exactly once, as `All proceeds go towards charity 🤍🌎` on a ticketed party post, and never in the competitive event or results messages. That is an observed pattern, not necessarily the right one. For welfare campaigns and any message where the NGO credibility question is live, lead with where the money goes rather than tacking it on.

MUN and academic formats carry more formality in the detail drop and the day-before reminder. Dress code, reporting time, and the lateness consequence become non-negotiable inclusions.

## Pre-send checklist

Word count inside its band. At least one line exists purely for tone. Could not have been sent verbatim by any other org in Kolkata. Register correct for the audience. Lifecycle stage skeleton followed. No setup sentence before the announcement. Anything that explains rather than announces has been cut or split into its own message. Bold on names, prices, deadlines, and the one action. Links bare, on their own line, correct depth. Emoji count within range for the register. Blank lines between paragraphs. No em dashes. No bullets where prose belongs. Runner-up named if results. Redirect present if refusal. Error path pre-empted if delivering something. Exactly one `*Please Note:*` if rules are involved. Signature matches register. Event descriptor reflects the event's actual content, not an invented label.
