# Lantern Taxonomy

An art movement of patient enumeration. It holds that a gathering is best understood the way a naturalist understands a field: by laying specimens side by side on a quiet ground, giving each the same measure of attention, and letting the rhythm of repetition do the arguing. Nothing is explained. Everything is counted, spaced and ruled, until the accumulation itself becomes the feeling.

Space is a dark, still field, and light is the only thing allowed to be loud. The ground stays near black so that a small number of warm marks can carry the whole emotional weight of the piece. Margins are generous and exact. Each specimen lives inside a cell of identical proportion, so that difference reads as character rather than noise. The grid is not decoration; it is the discipline that makes tenderness legible. Every alignment is the result of countless small corrections, the work of someone who has spent years learning where a hairline belongs.

Colour is rationed like a scarce pigment. One saffron-gold does the singing, a deeper ember is allowed a single appearance per passage, and a warm paper-white draws the structure. Everything else is shadow. Colour never fills a field; it punctuates, the way a single lit window defines a dark building. The palette should feel mixed by hand and tested against the ground many times, the product of deep expertise and painstaking attention rather than preference.

Scale moves in two registers only: the monumental sweep of a single curve, and the intimate tick of a small repeated mark. Between them is silence. Rhythm comes from spacing that is measured, never eyeballed, so that an eye travelling across the sheet feels a pulse before it finds a subject. Marks are drawn, not stamped: strokes of consistent weight, circles that are true, curves that settle into their sag with the inevitability of weight and gravity. Each repeated form is a meticulously crafted variation on its neighbours, never a copy.

Type is a footnote that has earned its place. A hairline mono for indices and rulers, a single italic serif phrase held back and set small, nothing that shouts. Words behave as labels on a specimen sheet: they identify, they never narrate. Text sits in its own clear air, never touching a mark, never nearing an edge. The finished sheet should look as though it were drawn over many nights by a master of the form, every detail refined until nothing could be moved without loss.
