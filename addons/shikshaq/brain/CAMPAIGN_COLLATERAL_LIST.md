## 1. Who we talk to, and the mechanism

Three audiences, one chain. **The student is the messenger.** A student sees the story, understands Shikshaq, then tells a parent. So the campaign has a layer built to be shown to parents, forwarded to parents and said out loud to parents, and it speaks to the student in the student's own voice.

| Audience | Belief to remove | Belief to install | What we ask |
|---|---|---|---|
| Students (Classes IV to XII) | "This is a charity class" | "This is where I find my tutor, and I can show it to Ma and Baba" | Search, then forward |
| Parents | "Another coaching centre" or "not for us" | "I search, read, and message the tutor myself. It costs me nothing to use" | Search by subject, class, board, locality |
| Tutors | "Volunteer teaching for an NGO" | "Free to list, set my own rate, real people message me" | List, or recommend a tutor |

**Origin line, used once per series:** "Made by AquaTerra, an NGO whose team are students." This is the site's own sentence (About page), so it is a recorded fact.

## 3. Design system: ten looks, all inside Shikshaq tokens

Every look uses the bone ground (never white), saturated slabs at radius 32, the weight-400 plus weight-800 headline pair, ring outlines (no borders), at most two saturated fills per poster, and the accent mapping from the site: orange for teachers and tutors, indigo for papers and the how-it-works layer, mint for answers. Your references supply the mechanisms. Their palettes and fonts stay out.

**Characters.** The five site blobs (rough, meh, fine, good, great) are the base. I extend the family with new flat shapes, a burst (sun) shape and a four-lobe X shape, built the same way: arch or lobed body, two dots, one mouth, no gradients, mood carries the meaning. The new characters get named in the first character story so they stay consistent across four weeks.

**Adaptations I am recording now:** (1) V2 puts several tilted stickers on one poster, where the site's own guidance allows one per card. That guidance is for cards in a grid, and a sticker pile is a different object, so I follow the reference and keep each sticker clear of the headline. (2) Your references use hand-drawn scribbles. You chose blobs only, so annotations in V1 and V4 become typed tags and highlighter blocks. (3) The references use white and cool greys. Everything here sits on warm bone.
