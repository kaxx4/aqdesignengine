# Faint low-opacity background fields read as dirt

**Seen in:** friendship_day carousel (2026-08-02), all six line slides + both bookends

**Encoded guard:** none — this is a **composition rule**, enforced by the [[03 The Looking Gate|looking gate]]

## The failure

To answer the perennial "too empty" problem ([[Dead half the reference fills]]), the first pass
reached for background *fields*: concentric rings, diagonal hatch, and checkerboards, all rendered
at **0.13–0.26 opacity** behind the content.

At those values, on both cream and ink, none of them read as design. They read as **stains** —
smudges, print defects, a dirty scan. Worse, they ran underneath body copy: on one slide the word
"asked," sat directly on a pale checker smear and became genuinely harder to read.

The pixel critique did not object. Fill percentages went up, which is exactly what the density
metrics reward. The pieces measured better and looked worse.

## Why it happens

Low opacity is the lazy answer to "add density without competing with the content." But the AQ
craft language is built on **hard edges** — thick ink outlines, hard-offset shadows, flat fills.
A 20%-opacity wash has no edge at all, so it cannot participate in that language. It reads as an
absence of intent.

This connects to the session-9 finding in [[MEASURED REFERENCE TARGETS (44 refs analyzed) — aim the showcase at these]]:
large flat colour regions lose both detail and area at once. A faint wash is the same mistake with
the contrast turned down.

## The rule

**Decoration is SOLID, ink-outlined, and hard-shadowed — never a low-opacity wash.**

In the v2 rebuild every faint field was deleted and replaced with a single solid **art panel**: a
flat accent plate with a 7px ink border, a 14px hard offset shadow, and a slight rotation, bleeding
off the right canvas edge. One confident shape did what six washes could not.

Corollary, consistent with [[LAYERING SYSTEM (the fix for too empty) — how the board fills space]]:
fill space by **scaling existing elements up** or by adding one committed plate — not by layering
translucent texture behind everything.
