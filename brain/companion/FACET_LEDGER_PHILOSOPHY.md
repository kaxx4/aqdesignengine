# FACET LEDGER

*Companion art piece for the TerraThon x Disco Diwali ticket poster. Plate: `dd_tickets_facets.png`. Builder: `scratchpad/tt_companion_facet_ledger.py`.*

## The movement

Facet Ledger is a way of looking at light as a record. A sphere of small mirrors is not a shape but a table of answers, one per tile, each asking the same question of the same single source: do you face it, and how squarely. The work treats brilliance as a measurement rather than an effect, and lets the measurement be beautiful.

## Space and form

The form is a single orthographic sphere, held dead centre on a black field, tiled 22 by 44 and cut at the horizon so only the near half speaks. Tiles are flat facets with a hairline of dark between them, the way real mirror tile is laid. Nothing else competes: a wide margin of black, then a thin ring of ticks at a fixed distance, then the edge of the page. The emptiness is part of the instrument.

## Colour and rule

Colour is never chosen tile by tile. One light sits upper left, and each tile takes the colour of its brightness band: cream where it meets the light head on, then green, blue, orchid, and finally an unlit outline of deep navy. The diagonal sweep from cream to orchid to nothing is the whole composition, and it emerges from the rule alone. Every joint, every taper toward the rim, is the consequence of geometry, executed with the patience of someone who has laid every one of these tiles by hand.

## Scale and rhythm

Rhythm comes from foreshortening. Tiles are generous and square at the centre and thin to slivers at the rim, so the eye reads curvature without a single shaded gradient. The ring of ticks answers with a second, slower rhythm: a short mark, four times, then a long one. Meticulously spaced, it turns the sphere into a dial.

## Text

Words are rare and clinical. A title and two counts, set small in a monospaced face at the foot of the page, read like a plate caption in an imaginary discipline. The counts are computed, never typed: the number of lit faces comes from the same loop that draws them.

## Craft

Every edge is drawn at twice the final resolution and reduced, so the hairlines stay crisp. The piece should read as the product of countless hours of calibration by a master of the form, where nothing is decorative and each mark can be traced to a rule.

## The quiet reference

The ring holds 55 ticks, every fifth one long, eleven in all. Fifty-five tens make the price of a pass. Only someone who counts will find it.
