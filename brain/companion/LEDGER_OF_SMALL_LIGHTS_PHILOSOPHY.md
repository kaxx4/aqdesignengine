# Ledger of Small Lights

*Companion philosophy for `brain/companion/welfare_ledger.png` (script: `scratchpad/tt_companion_welfare_ledger.py`).*

**Ledger of Small Lights** is the belief that a record, kept faithfully, is already beautiful. Each unit of work is one identical mark, set down once, never enlarged for drama and never hidden for modesty. The movement distrusts the hero element and trusts accumulation: meaning arrives when a thousand equal things stand in a row and the eye, left alone, finds the shape of what happened.

Space is a baseline and gravity. Every column rests on one hairline and grows upward in strict order of time, so height is the only argument the composition makes and it makes it silently. Between the columns the black is wide and unbothered. Negative space is not emptiness here; it is the field in which the unmarked world waits, and its size is what gives each small mark its weight. The grid is exact to the pixel, the pitch never varies, and the gaps between years are a half step, because a pause should be felt and not announced. Every alignment is the residue of countless refinements.

Colour carries time and nothing else. A cool beginning, a green middle, a warm present: three hues, one per era, so a column reads like a core sample, oldest at the floor and newest at the crown. The ground is true black so that each mark glows as if it were a light left on in a window. Nothing blends, nothing fades, nothing is decorated. The palette is small on purpose, and every instance of it was chosen with the patience of someone calibrating by eye for hours.

Typography is a surveyor's annotation: small monospaced capitals, set once, aligned to the columns they describe, quiet enough to be read as part of the instrument rather than as a caption. Numbers appear with leading zeros, as in a register. There are no sentences. The words that exist are labels, counts and a single line of provenance, because a plate that cites its own source earns the right to be trusted.

The work must look meticulously crafted, the product of deep expertise and painstaking attention, as though a master draughtsman placed each mark by hand and then checked it twice. Nothing touches the edge, nothing overlaps, and every spacing decision survives close inspection. The viewer should be able to stand at arm's length and see a skyline, then step closer and discover that the skyline is made of individual, countable, identical acts.
