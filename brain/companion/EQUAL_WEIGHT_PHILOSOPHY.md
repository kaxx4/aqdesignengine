# Equal Weight

Equal Weight is the belief that difference becomes legible only after everything else has been made fair. A hue
speaks clearly once brightness, saturation and size have stopped competing with it, and the work of the artist
is the long, quiet labour of removing every advantage except the one being studied. The result looks effortless
precisely because countless hours of calibration have gone into it. Nothing is loud; everything is exact.

Space is organised as a measured field, not a composition. Identical cells accumulate in a patient lattice,
each one the same size, the same radius, the same breath of gap from its neighbours, so the eye reads the
column and the row before it reads any single mark. The grid is not decoration but instrument: a place where
variation is permitted along exactly one axis at a time. This is meticulously crafted work, the product of deep
expertise, and every alignment should feel as though it survived a hundred small corrections.

Colour is held to a single discipline. A family of hues, drawn from one warm ground, is walked down a controlled
ladder of lightness, so that a row of cells shares a weight even as its colours differ completely. One rung of
that ladder is quietly distinguished, marked rather than shouted, the way a surveyor marks a datum. The palette
is limited and intentional; it earns its richness through repetition, and the whole must read as the result of
painstaking attention by someone at the very top of their field.

Typography is clinical and sparse: a thin, wide display voice for the single title, a small, even text face for the
numerals that index the axes. Words behave as instrument labels, never as explanation. They sit in the margins with
generous clearance, nothing overlaps, nothing touches the edge, and the type is treated as part of the apparatus of
observation. Master-level execution shows in the restraint: a line is added only if the plate would be wrong without it.

Rhythm comes from accumulation. Dozens of near-identical elements, each placed with the care of hand-set type, build
a surface that rewards sustained looking and reveals its single idea slowly. Balance is achieved by weight that is
genuinely equal, not by symmetry for its own sake. The finished plate should feel like a page from an imaginary
discipline, a study of how fairness is manufactured, executed with the patience and polish of work meant for a museum wall.
