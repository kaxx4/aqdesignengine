# RECRUITMENT NARRATIVES, RESEARCH AND VARIATIONS (2026-11-13)

Status: strategy draft for the owner to choose from. No posters are built from this yet. Owner brief: "students as a word is getting repetitive, stop designing and frame the narrative with me", then "build our variations, do research".

Evidence labels used below: **[fact]** counted in an AQ source, **[view]** widely held in outside research, **[inference]** my reading, **[unknown]** not established. Individual applicants are never named here, many are minors, and applicant lines are paraphrased.

## 1. WHAT THE RESEARCH SAYS

### 1a. Inside AQ (the strongest evidence, because it is yours)

**Demand is lopsided.** The AQ Departments Recruitment Form (Drive, responses sheet) has one tab per team. Row counts, minus the header, which is applications received and not unique people:

| Team tab | Applications |
|---|---|
| Social Media | 421 |
| Welfare Campaigns | 226 |
| Teaching Internship | 192 |
| Worksheets | 105 |
| Collabs and Outreach | 52 |
| Ventures (AQ.Ventures, marketing for student businesses) | 35 |
| Website Dev | 9 |

[fact] The period is not stated; the form was created Nov 2025 and last edited Oct 2026.

**Supply is short in exactly the thin lanes.** The 2025 Year End Review (Drive) names the problems each team had:
- Projects: "getting volunteers for workshops and drives" was difficult; content from on-ground volunteers arrived late. [fact]
- Ventures: "limited availability of skilled editors"; outreach to student businesses was "not scaled adequately". [fact]
- Social: output stalls in exam season when nobody in a sub-team is active. [fact]
- Collabs: over-commitment and uneven workload. [fact]
- Blogs launched Nov 2025 and built an editorial board "from the ground up"; visuals are made "without the use of AI, ensuring all published material remains entirely human-created". [fact]

**Proof points that are usable with their qualifiers** (2025 Year End Review, per team): HR inducted 500+ people and issued 30+ certificates, LORs and LOVs; Social had two months above 1.1M views and an acting reel at 180K+ views; Collabs worked with 6 schools, 2 colleges, 16 NGOs, 7 influencers and 5 college clubs, and ran drives for 200+ school students in their own schools and 400+ children with NGOs; Ventures supported 5 student-led businesses and 3 of them set up stalls at AQ events; Projects ran a 2,500 kg donation drive, 1,000 kg of clothes with Goonj, a 15,000 banana drive and 254 sanitary kits. [fact, 2025 scope]

**How applicants write today.** The platform's opening applications are few (15 expressions of interest, 2 applications, so [unknown] how representative). Paraphrased patterns:
1. Generic cause language: "something bigger", "an actual impact on society". [fact, sample of 1]
2. Wanting the real thing: "building things people actually use", "a real site with real readers", "how a team ships on a deadline". [fact, sample of 1]
3. A skill offer with no cause attached: "good at designing posts", "reels and marketing". [fact, sample of 2]
[inference] The generic cause language is what every NGO ad has taught teens to say. The specific, concrete wanting is the stronger signal and it is the one our copy should speak to.

### 1b. Outside AQ (search summaries only; I did not open the full papers, so strength varies)

- Motives for teen volunteering are mixed, and **competence and belonging recur** more than any other pair. In a high-school service-learning study, competence and relatedness predicted wanting to serve again; autonomy was less clearly tied to persistence. A study of 803 college volunteers found competence and relatedness, not autonomy, associated with sustained volunteering. Leadership roles were linked to persistence. [view; adolescent-specific evidence is thin] ([NHSJS review](https://nhsjs.com/2025/leading-giving-back-and-self-development-what-powers-the-volunteer-drive/), [PMC study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8701654))
- **Word of mouth is the main recruitment route** for youth volunteering in Irish, Canadian and UK studies, and friends are a strong pull; groups and peer cohorts help retention. A source I could not verify claims half of US teen volunteers do it because friends do. Peer pressure can also work against joining. [view] ([UCC Ireland report](https://www.ucc.ie/en/media/research/iss21/PromotingYouthVolunteeringinIreland.pdf), [UK youth sector summary](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/volunteering-in-the-youth-sector-research/volunteering-in-the-youth-sector-executive-summary))
- **Gen Z recruitment guidance** from employer-branding writers: authenticity beats polish; state demanding conditions up front because the complaint is "being sold one thing and experiencing another"; Instagram and TikTok outrank websites for discovery. Sources are employer-side and aimed at older students, so transfer to teens is [inference]. ([Red Pepper](https://redpepper.land/blog/recruiting-gen-z-3-insights-from-our-work-on-employer-branding-campaigns), [Recruiteze](https://recruiteze.com/?p=110996))
- **India:** I found no Indian survey of school students' volunteering motives. A discussion of certificate culture argues certificates become the goal and cheapen the experience. [unknown for AQ's audience; use as a caution] ([IndiaBioscience](https://indiabioscience.org/columns/education/in-a-rat-race-for-certificates))

### 1c. What this adds up to [inference]

1. **Competence is the hook, belonging is the retention.** "You'd be trusted with a real thing" gets attention; "you'd do it with people" keeps them.
2. **Peers recruit better than posters.** Every asset should be built to be forwarded to one friend, and the WhatsApp tier matters more than the feed.
3. **Be honest about the cost.** Unpaid, exam-season gaps, a real Sunday commitment. Honesty fits both the research and AQ's dry voice.
4. **Do not lead with certificates.** They are real (HR issued 30+), but the research on certificate culture and your own "the perk is not being on the sidelines" both point away.
5. **Steer demand.** Social media gets about 40 times the applications Website Dev gets. A generic push adds to the oversubscribed lane. A good campaign names the open seats.
6. **Cause language is saturated.** Applicants already recite "make an impact". Concrete tasks beat it.

## 2. THE VARIATIONS

Each is a narrative spine, not a poster. Sample lines use only facts above, the cleared ShikshAQ facts (Kolkata, classes IV to XII, boards, shikshaq.in) and no member names. Voice: dry, lowercase body, no em dashes, no Bengali.

| # | Narrative | The idea | Events | Welfare | Crftd | WhatsApp line |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Notes on feasibility: A, B, D, E, F use only existing facts. G needs no new facts but is the only one that risks "judging the audience" (rule 6 in the master brief), so it must stay playful. H is a lane-level texture, not a spine. I is the highest-leverage and the only one that needs the owner's say on what to reveal about application counts.

### Recommended combination [inference]

- **Spine: A, "Somebody has to."** It's the shortest, it never says "student" or "impact", and the ask is built in. Photos carry the age.
- **Recurring unit: B, "Now hiring" with honest terms.** It makes the pieces feel native to the feed, it matches the site's own "unpaid, zero fees, no experience needed", and it matches the research on honesty.
- **Direction: I.** Weight the content toward the open seats (website, editing, collabs, drives, workshop volunteers) so the campaign adds to the thin lanes and not the oversubscribed one.
- **Mechanism: C.** Every WhatsApp asset ends with "send this to one friend."
- **Proof, not spine: D, E, F.** Use them as the one fact under each headline.
- **Texture: H** on blogs, design and ShikshAQ.
- **Pace: G** for stories only.

### Risks

- A and B are jokes and B could read as mocking unpaid work. It works only if the "perks" are real and the pay line is plainly true.
- I reveals which lanes are thin. Say "open seats", never the counts.
- Everything on this page rests on one internal data source for demand and thin outside evidence on teens. Treat the claims as hypotheses.

## 3. HOW TO TEST WITHOUT INVENTING NUMBERS

No baseline exists for any metric, so no targets are set. Cheap test, two weeks, no new design needed:
1. Run A, B and G as three story polls ("which would you actually open?") and compare votes.
2. Send A and C as two forwardable WhatsApp texts to two comparable class groups and compare forwards and link clicks.
3. Record application counts per team before and after, to see whether "open seats" moves the thin lanes.
