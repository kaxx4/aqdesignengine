# Edge of the Frame

A movement about what a frame hides. A single wide scene is drawn once as a long band, and then ten narrow windows are cut from it, each at a different station along its length, so that the same night is read ten times from ten places. The scene never changes; only the place you stand in it does.

Form is a long horizontal band and ten tall slits of identical width, each outlined in a hairline and set at its true position over the band. Every slit is meticulously crafted and placed to the pixel, the product of painstaking attention, so that the viewer can read exactly which part of the whole each window admits.

Colour is sampled from the real stories: each slit holds a vertical gradient taken from its night, and the band behind holds the soft average of all ten. The ground is near-black and the hairline is a single warm gold, the only constant. This restraint is the work of someone at the top of their craft who knows that the difference between ten crops is told by where they sit, not by what is added.

Rhythm is the position of the slits. They are unevenly spaced on purpose, clustered where the scene is dense and sparse where it opens, and the unevenness is calibrated with master-level patience so it reads as nature, not accident.

Type is a whisper: a small tracked index under each slit, and one legend at the head. The sheet should look labored over for countless hours, a survey of a single night taken from its edges.
