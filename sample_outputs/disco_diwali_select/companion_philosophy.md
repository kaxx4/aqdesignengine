# One Chosen Line

A movement about choosing. A field of identical lines is laid down, each a thin dim stroke, and from among them one is lifted: marked at its two ends by a pair of handles and set on a pale band. Everything else stays dim. The sheet is the act of selection, rendered as a drawing.

Form is the line, the band and the handle. Lines are short, evenly stacked and unevenly long, like text with the words removed; the chosen line is wrapped in a band of one pale value, and a gold dot on a stem marks each end. Every edge is meticulously crafted and the product of painstaking attention, so the single chosen line reads as decided rather than accidental.

Colour is rationed to three values on a near-black ground: a dim off-white for the unchosen, one pale cream for the band, and a single warm gold for the handles. The restraint is the work of someone at the top of their craft who knows that emphasis is made by what is left quiet.

Space is the field. The lines sit at a constant pitch, so the eye reads the chosen one as a break in rhythm, and the margins are measured with master-level patience until the selection sits where the weight of the sheet wants it.

Type is a whisper: one small tracked caption at the foot, set like a legend. The sheet should look labored over for countless hours, a diagram of a single decision.
