# Two Rows

A movement about repetition across two registers. A single structure is drawn ten times in a line, and then the line is drawn again beneath itself, and the eye learns to read the first row as the question and the second as the answer. The pairing is the work: the same rhythm, a different sequence of lights, held at an exact distance from each other.

Form is a narrow tall frame with a hairline edge and almost nothing inside it, a small block near the top and a single bar near the base. The skeleton is reduced to what survives at a glance, so that colour carries the difference and structure carries the sameness. Every frame is meticulously crafted and placed to the pixel, the product of painstaking attention, so that two rows of ten read as one measured system rather than as twenty separate objects.

Colour is sampled, never invented. Each frame holds a vertical gradient taken from a real night, a warm head fading to a cooler floor, and the two rows are allowed to disagree in hue while agreeing in value. The ground is near-black and the hairline is a single gold, the only constant colour on the sheet. This restraint is the work of someone at the top of their craft who knows that difference only reads against sameness.

Rhythm lives in the gutters. The pitch between frames is identical across both rows, and the gap between the rows is wider than the gap within them, so that each row holds together as a sentence and the two sentences stay distinct. Balance is horizontal and quiet: nothing is favoured, nothing touches the edge, and the margins are measured with master-level patience.

Type is a whisper: two small tracked labels, one at the head of each row, set like the legend on a specimen plate. The sheet should look labored over for countless hours, an instrument for comparing two ten-night sequences.
