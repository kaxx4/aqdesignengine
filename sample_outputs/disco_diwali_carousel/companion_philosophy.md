# Stacked Light

A movement about repetition that refuses to finish. One frame, drawn again and again, each copy sinking a little further back, each one interrupted by the next. Nothing is shown whole. The eye reconstructs what the frame hides, and that act of reconstruction is the picture.

Form is a single trapezoid, a rectangle tilted away from the viewer until its far edge narrows by a fixed, patient ratio. The same ratio governs every copy. Space is the interval between copies: constant, mechanical, almost cruel, so that any deviation reads as a held breath. The ground is near-black and absorbs everything that is not a line.

Colour is rationed. Off-white carries structure, drawn thin and exact. Gold appears once in a measured count, a pulse at a regular beat, never decoration. The restraint is the luxury. Every edge is the product of meticulous, painstaking calibration; the work should look labored over for countless hours by someone at the very top of their craft.

Scale rewards patience. From across a room the stack is one rhythm; up close each frame has its own occlusion, its own shadow of the next. Typography is clinical and nearly silent: tiny tracked labels, figure numbers, one phrase set like a specimen caption. Master-level execution lives in the alignments nobody consciously checks.

The reference is never announced. A viewer who knows the night will feel a ceiling of mirrored light and a crowd seen through a sequence of panes. Anyone else sees a flawless diagram of something repeating into the dark.
