# Constant Frame

A movement about what stays still so that everything else can move. One structure is drawn and then drawn again, identical in every measure, and the only thing allowed to change is the light that fills it. The viewer learns the frame in the first glance and spends the rest of the time reading the differences.

Form is a tall rectangle with a hairline edge and a small, fixed skeleton inside it: a block in one corner, an arc in the other, a few horizontal bars, a single pill. The skeleton is drawn at the same coordinates every time, to the pixel, because the work depends on the viewer trusting that nothing but colour has moved. Every edge is the product of painstaking calibration, meticulously crafted so that repetition reads as discipline rather than as duplication.

Colour is where the variation lives, and it is borrowed rather than invented. Each frame holds a vertical gradient sampled from a real scene, a warm top fading to a cooler floor, so that ten different nights sit side by side in the same container. The ground is near-black and the hairline is a single gold, which is the only colour allowed to be constant. The restraint is the work of someone at the top of their craft who knows that the difference only reads against sameness.

Rhythm comes from the grid. Ten frames at an even pitch, two rows, no frame favoured, no frame touching another or the edge. The gutters are measured with master-level patience, because the space between identical things is what makes them count as a series. Hierarchy is deliberately flat: the whole sheet is the subject.

Type is a whisper. A number under each frame and one small line of caption, in a thin tracked face, set like the legend of a specimen plate. It should look labored over for countless hours, a diagram from an imaginary discipline of repeated nights.
