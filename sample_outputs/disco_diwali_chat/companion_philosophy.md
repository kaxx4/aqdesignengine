# Call and Answer

A movement about rhythm in exchange. Two voices alternate, one from the left edge and one from the right, and the sheet records nothing of what they say, only the shape of the saying: a short mark answered by a long one, a long one answered by a short, the pattern of a conversation that is going somewhere.

Form is the bar, a rounded stroke of fixed height and variable length, set against a near-black ground. Left bars are pale and short, right bars are long and carry the single saturated colour on the sheet. Every edge is meticulously crafted, the product of painstaking attention, so that the rhythm reads as composed rather than random.

Colour is rationed to three values: off-white for the asking side, one clear blue for the answering side, and a thin gold hairline that ends the exchange. The restraint is the work of someone at the top of their craft who understands that a single saturated colour carries more than five.

Space does the speaking. The vertical interval between bars is constant, so the eye reads the stagger as tempo, and the margins are measured with master-level patience until the left and right edges weigh the same.

Type is a whisper: one small tracked label at the foot, like the signature on a plate. The sheet should look labored over for countless hours, a score for a conversation.
