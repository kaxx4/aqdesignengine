# Last Seat

A movement about arrival at the limit. A grid of identical seats is drawn, row on row, and every one of them is taken, filled in the same warm tone, until the final seat is reached and marked. The sheet is the instant a count stops.

Form is the seat, a small rounded square repeated at a constant pitch, with a single hairline frame around the whole block like the walls of a room that can hold no more. Every seat is meticulously crafted and placed to the pixel, the product of painstaking attention, so that the repetition reads as capacity rather than decoration.

Colour is two values on a near-black ground: the filled seat in a soft gold and the marked last seat in off-white, outlined in gold. The restraint is the work of someone at the top of their craft who understands that fullness is shown by the absence of any empty cell, not by any added flourish.

Space is the grid and nothing else. The gutters are identical in every direction, the block sits centred with generous margins, and the weight is balanced with master-level patience until the one marked seat, set off the diagonal, becomes the quiet focal point.

Type is a whisper: one small tracked caption under the block, set like the legend on a seating plan. The sheet should look labored over for countless hours, a diagram of a room at capacity.
