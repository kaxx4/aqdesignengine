# Held Open

A movement about the thing that is not there. A structure is drawn completely, every part in its place, and then one part is withdrawn and the space it occupied is left open and marked. The viewer sees the whole by seeing the gap, and the gap becomes the loudest element on the sheet without a single added mark.

Form is a frame and a skeleton: a block, an arc, a few bars of different length, all in fine off-white line, and one outline drawn in a broken gold stroke where the missing piece belongs. The broken line is the only act of emphasis. Every edge is meticulously crafted, the product of painstaking attention, so that the omission reads as a decision made by someone at the top of their craft rather than as an accident of editing.

Colour is two values on a near-black ground, the off-white of the structure and the single warm gold of the absence. Nothing else is permitted to be gold, so that the viewer's eye finds the gap before it finds anything else. The restraint is the work of countless hours of subtraction, the discipline of knowing which element earns its place.

Space is the material. The open region is held by measured margins on every side, wider than the rest of the sheet's gutters, so that it breathes the way a rest breathes in music. Balance comes from the weight of the gap against the weight of everything that remains, calibrated with master-level patience until neither side wins.

Type is a whisper: one small tracked label set against the gap like the legend on a survey plate. The sheet should read as an instrument for measuring what has been left out, and it should look labored over for countless hours.
