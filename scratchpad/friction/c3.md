# Friction log — Workflow C, landscape canvas (c3, "126 return visits...", linkedin 1200x628)

Final version: `out/session10f/c3_v3.png`, built by `scratchpad/c3_v3.py` (v1/v2 kept alongside it,
each with an inline docstring explaining exactly what was wrong and what changed).

Verdict up front: genuinely good, not just clean-scoring. Static preflight is CLEAN, the DOM audit
is CLEAN, and the looking gate (Sec 3) passes on every listed principle — legibility, hierarchy
(the wordmark is the one unmissable hero, columns are equal-scale secondaries), balance (all four
corners occupied, no dead quadrant), rhythm (three identical pill/value/caption columns, five
stickers spaced at even fractions across the wordmark), contrast (measured: white 4.95:1 and cream
4.30:1 on the teal panel, both pass AA), and craft (ink outlines + hard-offset shadows throughout).
Two advisory prints remain and are BOTH confirmed false positives, not swept under the rug — see
"gates that cried wolf" below. One deliberate omission (no hero-shine) is also recorded, not hidden.

---

## 1. Was the style brief usable at this aspect, or was re-proportioning pure guesswork?

**Half and half, and the half that was guesswork is the important finding.**

`design.py`'s brief gives you: the mechanism (prose), the MEASURED numbers (`compare.geometry`
on the raw reference file), and a `canvas` field. What it does NOT give you — and what the task
brief's framing ("re-proportion a 4:5 portrait") assumes exists somewhere — is any measured
number that is actually about the TARGET canvas. Concretely:

- The reference this seed drew (`7d4fa0d720fa7f49dded1e854599c4e0.jpg`) is not a portrait at all.
  It's 1200x900 (4:3), and it isn't even a native poster — it's a screenshot of a website's
  footer with a browser-card floating on a lavender backdrop. `compare.geometry` measured
  margins/coverage on the WHOLE 4:3 frame including that backdrop (content bbox y 0.154..0.844,
  margins ~15.5% top/bottom), which is a real, correctly-measured number that is almost useless
  for a full-bleed 1.91:1 AQ poster, because the thing being measured (backdrop + card) is not the
  thing being built (the card's mechanism alone, full-bleed). I had to recognize that myself —
  nothing in the brief flags "this reference has an outer frame, measure the INNER card
  instead," and nothing automates re-measuring just the card. Sec 5 step 0 has exactly this
  caveat for mockups ("phones/devices/print/a scene") with a `compare.crop` tool to handle it —
  but a website-screenshot-in-a-rounded-card is arguably the same class of problem and the
  manual's mockup language doesn't clearly cover it, so I made the call by eye rather than by rule.
  **Ambiguity to flag**: is a padded browser-card screenshot a "mockup" under Sec 5 step 0? If yes,
  the step-0 instruction ("crop the chosen screen FIRST, then measure the crop") should say so
  explicitly, because I would not have known to crop this one without independently deciding it.
- `canvas: linkedin` in the brief is a JUDGED field (`stylebank.py`'s `CANVAS_RULE`: "fit for
  purpose, not nearest aspect") meaning a human decided this MECHANISM works on a 1.91:1 canvas —
  it says nothing about how the mechanism's own internal proportions (column widths, wordmark
  height budget, top-strip fraction) should change to get there from the reference's native 1.6:1
  card. That re-proportioning is entirely unassisted. I ended up doing it by: (a) keeping the
  reference's top-strip/panel height SPLIT (~17%/83%) since that's a vertical proportion and our
  height didn't change much in absolute terms, and (b) giving the recovered EXTRA WIDTH entirely
  to breathing room in the 3-column block rather than to the wordmark (which was already
  full-width in the reference and stays full-width here). That's a reasoned decision, but it's
  MINE, not something the brief or CLAUDE.md pointed me toward, and a different (equally
  reasonable) build could have shrunk the top strip instead, or split the extra width
  differently. **Guidance I wanted and did not get**: some worked convention (even one sentence)
  for "which dimension of a reused mechanism is allowed to change when the target aspect differs
  from the reference's, and which must not" — right now this is 100% author judgment call, redone
  from scratch per piece.
- Net: the brief is usable as a CONCEPT (the mechanism, the department colour, the one real
  number) but not as a LAYOUT — every actual coordinate, gap, and font-size in this script came
  from ordinary build-and-measure work, exactly as if `design.py` had not specified a canvas at
  all. That's probably fine (Sec 2 already says design.py "prints a BRIEF; it does not render"),
  but the task's framing implied more re-proportioning guidance exists than actually does.

## 2. Manual gaps found — general, not just portrait-shaped

- **Sec 5 step-0 "mockup" definition is ambiguous** for a screenshot-in-a-card (see above) — not
  clearly in or out of the "crop first" instruction.
- **No re-proportioning guidance for Workflow C across aspect ratios**, beyond "build to the
  measured numbers" — which assumes the measured numbers are for the SAME aspect you're building.
  Sec 2's Workflow C description doesn't mention aspect mismatch at all.
- **No portrait-shaped landmine actually bit me in the gates I exercised** — `bounds_check`,
  `collision_check`, `dominance_check` (min_hero_frac is an AREA fraction of W*H, aspect-
  independent), and `preview.critique`'s quadrant split (a plain W/2 x H/2 split, works the same
  shape-wise on any aspect) are all genuinely aspect-neutral. I went in expecting to find a
  hardcoded 1080/1350 assumption per the task's warning and did NOT find one in anything I
  exercised. The one place a portrait number IS hardcoded and DOES leak into a landscape build
  (`audit.py`'s `M=64` module constant used for margin checks) is not "portrait-shaped" exactly —
  it doesn't scale with either axis, it would misfire the same way on ANY unusual canvas, tall or
  wide — so I'm reporting it under "APIs that surprised me" below rather than here, but it's the
  closest thing I found to what the task was watching for.
- **`ARCHETYPE_PROFILES`-style density/fill-floor exemptions exist ONLY for Workflow A's
  registered archetypes.** `preview.critique`'s `flat_dominant` (>52% one colour) fired on this
  piece (fill 0.77-0.78, one flat teal field) — CORRECTLY, in the sense that the panel really is
  >52% of the canvas — but the reference's OWN written recipe explicitly demands this: "the script
  and panel stay two flat tones" is not incidental, it's the style's stated restraint. Workflow A
  has a documented escape hatch for exactly this situation (`giant_type`'s dark base is exempted
  in `ARCHETYPE_PROFILES`); Workflow B/C bespoke scripts have no equivalent, so the check will
  ALWAYS flag a legitimately flat-panel mechanism with no way to tell "this is the recipe" from
  "this is lazy." I did not add texture to break up the field, because doing so would contradict
  the drawn style's own recipe — but this is a judgment call CLAUDE.md gives no support for either
  way, and I want it on record that I overrode a metric on purpose rather than silently ignoring
  a bad number.

## 3. APIs that surprised me

1. **`build.measure_text()` cannot measure italic text — silent, not documented as a limitation.**
   Its JS measurer builds the canvas font string as `cs.fontWeight + ' ' + cs.fontSize + ' ' +
   cs.fontFamily` (engine/build.py, inside the `_work` closure passed to `pg.evaluate`) — no
   `font-style`, and the `items` dict schema documented in the function's own docstring has no
   `style`/`italic` field at all. I measured "aquaterra" as regular Instrument Serif at 300px
   (`glyph_w: 1003`), used that to solve a font-size for a 1120px target, and the ACTUAL italic
   render came out 1231px wide — 111px (+9.9%) more than predicted, at a size where the div's
   declared box (from the same wrong prediction) also said 1120px. Nothing in `bounds_check`
   caught this because it operates on the DECLARED tuple, which was wrong in the same direction
   as the render. The only thing that caught it was `reconcile.measure_dom`'s OVERSIZE warning
   post-render (`"real width 1231px vs 1120px box — a hand-written bbox here under-reports by
   111px"`) — i.e. the gate worked exactly as advertised, but only AFTER a full render, and the
   wordmark had already bled 71px off the canvas's right edge in that draft. Given Instrument
   Serif's ENTIRE sanctioned use in the brand system is italic (Sec 9: "≤1 accent word per piece,"
   always italic), this is not an edge case — it's the normal way anyone would reach for this
   font, and the measurement primitive can't see it. Workaround used (documented in the v2
   docstring): throw away the measure_text prediction and calibrate directly off v1's real
   rendered numbers (`WORD_W_PER_PX = 1231/335`, linear through the origin since there's no
   wrapping or letter-spacing) — correct for THIS string+face, but it means every future italic
   hero has to rediscover this the same way, one bad render at a time, unless `measure_text`
   grows a `style` field.
2. **Two independent, non-cooperating overlap checkers.** `layout.preflight`'s tuple-based
   `collision_check` takes a `collision_ignore` param for by-design overlaps, and I used it. But
   `build.render()` ALSO auto-runs `audit.py`'s DOM-based overlap check on every render, and that
   one has its OWN whitelist (`SKIP_PAIRS`, a fixed set of same-tag pairs like `("word","word")`,
   `("stk","stk")`) that `collision_ignore` does not reach at all — I passed `collision_ignore` to
   both `layout.preflight(...)` and `B.render(...)`, and the five sticker/wordmark overlaps (glued
   onto its strokes BY DESIGN, the entire point of the reference's mechanism) still printed as
   `audit.py` "ISSUES" every time. `SKIP_PAIRS` only matches literal same-string tag pairs from a
   closed vocabulary baked into `engine/audit.py` — a bespoke script cannot add to it or
   parametrize it. The only thing that actually suppresses an `audit.py` overlap regardless of
   tag name is parent/child DOM nesting (`if nest[i]==j or nest[j]==i: continue`), which is not
   documented anywhere as "the way to declare an intentional overlap" — I found it by reading
   `audit.py`'s source after the ignore-param approach silently didn't work. Fix used in v3:
   nested the sticker `<div>`s as real children of the wordmark `<div>` instead of positioning
   them as page-level siblings. That worked, and is arguably the more honest DOM structure anyway
   — but discovering it required reading engine internals CLAUDE.md doesn't point to for this.
3. **`audit.py`'s margin check uses a bare module-level `M=64`, not derived from the canvas or
   from `build.py`'s own `M`.** Not aspect-broken (any canvas gets the same flat 64px inset,
   which is at least consistent) but it IS a second, independent margin constant living in a
   different file from `build.py`'s `M=64` — they happen to agree today, by coincidence of two
   separately-hardcoded numbers, not by one importing the other (`engine/audit.py` line ~3:
   `M=64; W,H=1080,1350` — literally redeclares canvas defaults too, unused here since I passed
   `canvas=(W,H)` explicitly, but a script that forgot to pass `canvas` to `B.render` would get
   silently audited against 1080x1350 regardless of its real size). My wordmark grazed this by
   exactly 1px on each side (`x60 r1140` vs a `M-3=61 .. aW-M+3=1139` safe window) — a threshold
   coincidence, not a real design flaw, but the FIRST time I saw it printed I could not tell the
   difference without doing the arithmetic myself, because the message (`"MARGIN wordmark
   breaches safe area (x60 r1140 b552)"`) doesn't say by how much or what the threshold was.
4. **`same_as_bg_scan` (auto-run in `build.render`) has no concept of intermediate surfaces.**
   It flags any inline `background:` declaration that resolves to (near-)exactly the PAGE bg,
   full stop — it cannot know that an element sits on top of a differently-coloured panel and is
   completely visible there. My three pill labels (`background:{CREAM}`, sitting on the TEAL
   panel — cream-on-teal is the correct, legible, house-recommended choice, verified against
   `text_on`) tripped this on every render: `⚠ FILL SAME AS PAGE BG (element invisible):
   [('#F4EFE0', '#f4efe0', 0.0)]`. Confirmed false positive by reading `layout.same_as_bg_scan`'s
   source directly (it explicitly skips full-bleed backdrops via an `inset:0`/`100%x100%` check,
   but has no equivalent "what's actually behind this element" awareness for a normal
   arbitrarily-positioned div). This is a **gate that cried wolf** on the single most defensible
   colour choice in the whole piece — worth fixing (pass the LOCAL surface per-element, the way
   the opt-in `invisible_color_check`'s `color_pairs` already does, rather than one global page_bg)
   but not something I could fix from a bespoke script.
5. **`reconcile.measure_dom`'s "UNTRACKED div" advisory fires on any positioned element you
   deliberately did NOT add to `elements`** — in my case the teal panel rect, which I excluded on
   purpose (full-bleed background fills aren't "placed objects" in the tuple-world sense, and the
   CLAUDE.md template's own worked example never adds them either). The advisory reads exactly
   like something is wrong ("no gate ever considered it") when in this case nothing needed to.
   Not a bug, just a rough edge in how alarming an advisory-only line can sound out of context.

None of (3)-(5) blocked anything or produced a bad PNG — they're print-noise once you understand
them. (1) and (2) were the two that actually cost render iterations.

## 4. Iteration log — what each version cost, and what I had to guess

- **Setup (before v1, ~unmeasured but the bulk of the session's time)**: read `CLAUDE.md` in full,
  ran `design.py`, read the reference image, ran `compare.geometry`, then read
  `engine/core.py`, `engine/build.py`, `engine/doodles.py`, `engine/shapes.py`, and the relevant
  slices of `engine/layout.py` (`preflight`, `bounds_check`, `collision_check`,
  `dominance_check`, `contains_check`) because nothing in CLAUDE.md's landscape-specific guidance
  exists to shortcut this — Sec 6's template is written assuming `feed` (1080x1350) and I wanted
  to confirm every constant I was about to reuse (`M=64`, `min_hero_frac=0.12`, the quadrant
  split) was actually aspect-neutral before trusting it, which meant reading the source rather
  than the manual for each one.
- **v1**: one render. Failed the gate stack (5 real "collisions," an off-canvas wordmark, a
  false-positive panel-collision spam) and failed the looking gate on hierarchy (two competing
  heroes). Root causes: the italic measurement gap (API surprise 1) and an unforced layout
  decision (128px hero number) that turned out to be a hierarchy mistake once seen rendered, not
  before. I could not have caught either without rendering — the numbers looked plausible on
  paper.
- **v2**: diagnosed both root causes, rewrote the column sizing (equal-scale values, wordmark as
  sole hero) and re-derived the wordmark's font-size from v1's own real render instead of
  `measure_text`. Result: static `preflight` CLEAN, looking gate genuinely passes (this is the
  version whose PNG I'd ship if the two remaining audit-only prints didn't exist). Remaining
  noise: the two false-positive advisories (3)/(4) above plus the real-but-cosmetic 1px margin
  graze and the by-design sticker/wordmark overlaps that `audit.py` still flagged as issues.
- **v3**: no visual change at all (confirmed by re-reading the PNG side-by-side with v2 — same
  composition, same numbers to within a few px). Only change: nested stickers into the wordmark
  div (fixes the audit overlap noise) and trimmed the wordmark's target width 1080->1050px
  (clears the margin graze with real headroom instead of a 1px coincidence). Fully clean on both
  gate layers after this.
- **What I had to guess, honestly**: the top-strip/panel height split (108/520, i.e. ~17%/83%),
  the column gutter (40px), and the sticker size range (56-82px) are all judgment calls with no
  formula behind them — informed by the reference's own proportions and by what looked balanced
  once rendered, not derived from a rule. The wordmark's bottom-anchor padding (6px) is likewise
  eyeballed to match the reference's descenders grazing its panel edge.

## 5. One deliberate omission, on the record

Sec 3's craft checklist calls for "one hero-shine per piece"; this build has none. The reference's
own recipe explicitly asks for restraint ("the script and panel stay two flat tones") and I judged
that adding a gloss/shine to the wordmark would fight that restraint rather than serve it. CLAUDE.md
gives no guidance on which wins when a drawn style's own recipe conflicts with an "always-on" craft
rule — I made the call and am flagging it rather than silently either adding a shine that fights the
recipe or dropping the craft rule without saying so.
