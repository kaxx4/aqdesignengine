## 1. Docs: wrong, stale, ambiguous, or missing a step

- **`RECREATION_PROTOCOL.md`'s MOCKUP section is the closest guidance for an
  aspect-mismatched reference, but this isn't a mockup, and the protocol has no
  section for "same aspect-mismatch symptom, different (non-mockup) cause."** The
  MOCKUP section's decision table ends in "PARK it with `runqueue.py fail`." My task
  explicitly forbade that ("NOT a mockup... expect to reach an accepting score, not
  park it"). When my own control (same-aspect stacked reference) came back at 0.951 —
  barely different from the raw 0.903 — the protocol has literally no next step for
  "the control disproves the aspect theory and the score still won't move." I had to
  invent the diagnosis (isolate the slab, then trace to `content_mask`'s threshold)
  from scratch. This is a real gap: the protocol conflates "score doesn't match
  aspect" with "score won't move for aspect reasons," and only the second one has an
  exit ramp (PARK). A poster that is aspect-mismatched AND has a metric-sensitivity
  problem needs its own row in the decision table, or at minimum a pointer to
  `compare.py`'s own `content_mask`/`vdr` caveats (which DO exist, in CLAUDE.md §7c,
  but are never cross-referenced from RECREATION_PROTOCOL.md's scoring section).

- **The task brief's own framing ("this IS a poster... expect an accepting score")
  turned out to not hold, and nothing in the docs prepares an agent for that
  outcome.** I want to flag this without hedging: the premise handed to me was wrong
  for this specific slug, not just "hard." A same-aspect, same-content control still
  scored 0.951. If the grading process is going to keep asserting "expect to reach
  an accepting score" for slugs like this, either the assertion needs to be checked
  against a control BEFORE handing out the task, or the task needs to allow
  "attempted, score structurally capped" as a legitimate outcome up front instead of
  implicitly treating it as a task failure.

- **CLAUDE.md §6's template doodle-icon idiom is fine, but nothing in §6/§9 warns
  that nesting a `dd.stamp()` SVG (`width="100%" height="100%"`) inside a
  `display:flex; align-items:center` parent can resolve its percentage against the
  WRONG ancestor.** The doodle vocabulary section (§9) says "Call ONLY via
  `dd.stamp(kind, color, rot=, style=)`" and shows the flat `doodle()` helper (a
  plain absolutely-positioned div), but doesn't say "never wrap it in flex" — I only
  found this by deviating from the proven pattern and getting caught by
  `reconcile.measure_dom`. Worth a line in the bug catalog (§10): "SVG width=100%/
  height=100% inside a flex-centered wrapper resolves against the wrong ancestor,
  not the flex child's own box — use the plain absolutely-positioned div pattern
  instead."

- **Nothing in §7 or §9 warns that `build.logo()`'s hardcoded `z-index:20` will
  lose to any higher-z-index decoration placed on top of it (like a legibility
  fade).** This is worse than a cosmetic miss — the logo went from "legible" to
  "100% invisible, zero pixels" between v1 and v2, and NO gate caught it. See §3
  below for the full list of what should have caught this and didn't.

- **`brain/STYLE_BANK.json`'s recipe for this slug was genuinely good** (see §2) —
  no complaint there. But the recipe's optional-slab suggestion ("optionally pin one
  rotated ink slab... reads as laid over the pills") has a real, uncosted side
  effect the recipe doesn't mention: on THIS specific reference (whose own pill
  fill is nearly invisible to `content_mask`), adding a big solid-ink slab is
  disproportionately expensive in `compare.py`'s score (confirmed: removing it
  dropped the score from 0.951 to 0.775, ~18% of the total score value from one
  optional element). A style-bank recipe that recommends an addition should say
  what it costs on the metric side, or at least that it's metric-visible in a way
  the rest of the mechanism isn't.

## 2. Was the RE-PROPORTION guidance sufficient, or was I guessing?

**Mostly sufficient, with one real gap I had to fill by inference.** The recipe text
("Feed's taller portrait frame has far more vertical room... stack noticeably more
rows... roughly double the reference's row count... do NOT enlarge the pills
themselves") gave me the correct DIRECTIONAL instruction and I followed it literally
(kept pill height at the reference's own ~120-136px scale, let row count fall out of
`while y < H`, landed at ~9-10 rows vs the reference's 4, comfortably inside "roughly
double" territory and arguably beyond it, which the recipe explicitly endorses:
"more rows is the only correct way to fill a taller canvas").

**What I had to guess, with no textual guidance at all:**
- Whether the field should also bleed off the TOP and BOTTOM (vertical "endless"),
  not just left/right. The reference crop only shows 4 rows flush to its own frame
  edges, and the recipe never says whether that reflects a real top/bottom boundary
  in the source or is simply where the crop happens to end. I inferred "endless
  both axes" from the style bank's own tag list (`"endless"` is listed as a tag, not
  qualified to one axis) and from the fact that this is visibly a chat-UI screenshot
  (rows obviously continue off-screen in a real chat). This is a judgment call the
  recipe doesn't make for me, and a different reasonable agent could have built 4
  rows dead-centered with clean top/bottom margins instead — a materially different
  composition. The recipe should say which axis "endless" applies to.
  RECOMMENDATION: `brain/STYLE_BANK.json` recipes for `bleed-rows`/`endless`-tagged
  styles should state whether the bleed is one-axis or both, since I could not find
  this decided anywhere and it changes the whole silhouette of the piece.
- Whether the optional ink slab was actually a good idea for THIS slug specifically
  (given its metric cost, discovered only after building it — see §1). The recipe
  frames it as free/optional with no downside mentioned.
- The exact row/pill proportions to use (I kept the reference's absolute pill height
  ~136px rather than re-deriving it from any formula) — the recipe says "close to
  the reference's own absolute proportions" but gives no numeric anchor, so "136px"
  vs "120px" vs "150px" was my own call, unverified against anything but visual eye.

## 3. APIs that surprised me; gates that fired on nothing, or stayed silent on something real

- **`engine/audit.py`'s bleed-exemption vocabulary is a SEPARATE, hardcoded,
  non-extensible set from `layout.preflight`'s `bleed_tags` parameter, and this
  is not documented anywhere in CLAUDE.md.** `layout.py`'s `preflight(...,
  bleed_tags=(...))` lets the caller declare exactly which elements are allowed to
  bleed. `engine/audit.py` (a DIFFERENT gate, auto-run inside `build.render()`) has
  its own closed vocabulary: `BLEED={"num"}` at module scope (line 4 of
  `engine/audit.py`), plus a separate `MARGIN_OK={"note","key","flyer","we","won",
  "tb","title","body"}` whitelist — and its `audit()` function signature
  (`ignore_pairs`, `margin`) has **no parameter at all** to extend either set. If I
  had tagged my ~37 bleeding pills with `class="measure"` (the natural, "obviously
  correct" thing to do, since every other bespoke script in this codebase does
  that for every element), `audit.audit()` would have printed `MARGIN {tag}
  breaches safe area` for every single one, all correctly by-design, with **no way
  to suppress it short of literally renaming every pill's tag to the magic string
  `"num"`** — a hack I chose not to take. I found this only by reading
  `engine/audit.py`'s source directly (not by any doc), and worked around it by
  simply not giving the bleeding pills the `measure` class (they're still fully
  visible to `reconcile.measure_dom`'s broader selector, which DOES respect
  `bleed_tags`). This is worth a line in CLAUDE.md §7b: "audit.py's own
  bleed/margin exemptions are closed and cannot be extended by the caller; a
  bleed-mechanism piece should not tag its bleeding elements `class="measure"`."

- **`compare.report()` does not print — it returns a string.** I called
  `compare.report(ref, gen)` bare (per the exact pattern implied by CLAUDE.md
  §7c's description of the function) and got no output at all, no error either.
  Took a second call wrapped in `print()` to notice. Minor, but the silence (no
  output AND no error) is exactly the kind of thing that could make an agent think
  a report "ran clean" when it actually printed nothing.

- **`compare.py`'s `_load()` resizes BOTH images to a fixed `SIZE=(540,675)`
  (a 4:5 box) regardless of either image's own native aspect** (`engine/compare.py`
  line 42-46). The tool's own aspect-mismatch warning only compares the ref's
  aspect to the gen's aspect to EACH OTHER — it does not warn when both images
  share an aspect but that shared aspect differs sharply from the tool's own fixed
  4:5 analysis frame. I found this by accident while trying to build a "single
  row" fairness control: I cropped a single row from both ref (aspect ~6.9:1) and
  gen (aspect ~8:1) — a 14% mutual gap, under the 25% warn threshold, so `compare`
  printed no warning at all — and got SCORE 1.003, even worse than the whole-canvas
  comparison, because BOTH crops were still being force-squashed into a portrait
  4:5 box. **This is a gate that stayed silent on something real**: the warning's
  own logic (compare the two SOURCE aspects to each other) cannot catch the case
  where both sources agree with each other but disagree with the tool's own fixed
  frame. Worth fixing or at minimum documenting in `compare.py`'s docstring.

- **`invisible_color_check`'s default 40-unit threshold makes ANY near-white fill
  (every channel >=235) fail against AQ's own canonical `CREAM` token
  (#F4EFE0)** — I proved this by grid search (see `scratchpad/gen_62f8cc4d_v1.py`'s
  comments): pure white (#FFFFFF) is the mathematical MAXIMUM distance achievable
  from CREAM while staying "near-white" (36.6 units), still under the 40-unit
  threshold. This means **any AQ bespoke script that puts a plain white/paper card
  directly on the CREAM page background will hard-fail this check** the moment
  `color_pairs` includes that pairing — and I found that `scratchpad/
  gen_motifs_v6.py`'s own precedent piece (`piece_b`, the exact same
  "staggered bleed rows" mechanism) used `background:{PAPER}` directly on
  `{CREAM}` and NEVER hit this, only because it never passed `color_pairs` to
  `preflight()` for that combination. **This gate fired on something real (a
  provable, reproducible token conflict between two of AQ's own canonical brand
  colors) that had never actually been exercised by the one prior script using the
  identical combination.** I resolved it in my own script by using a light
  cool grey-blue (#D0D8E2, distance 42.8) for the card fill instead of pure white —
  but this is a standing, generalizable finding CLAUDE.md's engine team should
  know about: **PAPER-on-CREAM cannot pass `invisible_color_check` at the default
  threshold, full stop**, and every existing script relying on that combination is
  silently unverified on this exact axis.

- **A genuine, reproducible box-model bug**, not a false-positive: wrapping a
  `dd.stamp()` SVG (`width="100%" height="100%"`) inside a
  `display:flex;align-items:center;justify-content:center` parent, itself nested
  one level inside a `box-sizing:border-box` bordered circle, made the SVG's
  percentage resolve against the OUTER circle's content box (132px) rather than
  its immediate flex-item wrapper (68px) — `reconcile.measure_dom` caught this
  exactly right: `CLIPPED span: width 164px inside a 132px box — characters are
  lost` plus `CLIPPED {} (by span): 30px past its container's right/bottom edge`,
  repeated once per avatar (~37 times). It was VISUALLY invisible in the render
  (the outer circle's own `overflow:hidden` happened to mask the oversized icon
  at this specific avatar diameter) — an eye-only looking gate would have passed
  this and shipped a real bug that could clip for real at a smaller avatar size.
  This is the single clearest example in this session of the gate stack catching
  something the eye could not.

- **A genuine z-index bug my OWN fix introduced, caught only by re-zooming the
  render, not by any gate**: strengthening a legibility fade (`z-index:50`) over
  `build.logo()`'s hardcoded `z-index:20` made the AQ wordmark **100% invisible** —
  not faded, gone, zero pixels. NO check in §7 checks "does a foreground brand
  element still render above a decorative overlay" — not `invisible_color_check`
  (that's about fill vs. surface color, not stacking), not `occlusion_check` (it
  takes an explicit z-ordered list the caller must declare, and I hadn't declared
  the logo/fade pair to it), not `reconcile.measure_dom` (the logo still has a
  nonzero box — it's just painted with 0 opacity contribution, not clipped or
  off-canvas). **This is a real hole in the gate stack**: nothing checks "is this
  positioned/z-indexed element actually visible in the final paint," only "is it
  the right color" and "is it the right size/position." I only found it by
  re-zooming the exact same crop I'd used to diagnose the ORIGINAL legibility
  issue — i.e., only because I happened to re-verify my own fix by eye.

## 4. Where the SCORE disagreed with the EYE (both directions, with numbers)

- **Score said "badly wrong" (0.903-0.951), eye said "correctly matches, per the
  recipe's own explicit re-proportioning instructions."** Every element in the
  step-1 written inventory is present, correctly substituted per the real-assets
  rule, and correctly proportioned for the new aspect. `preview.critique` (the
  pixel-level proxy for the looking gate) is unambiguously clean: `fill 0.70,
  contrast 0.30`, all four quadrants within 0.69-0.71 (no dead quadrant, no
  flat_dominant, no sparse/crammed). The `compare.py` score disagrees by a huge
  margin (0.903 vs the 0.16 accept line) for reasons fully diagnosed and
  documented in `brain/RECREATION_AUDIT.md`: `content_mask`'s fixed 46-unit
  RGB-distance threshold is blind to the reference's own near-invisible pill fill
  (measured distance ~20 from its background) while registering AQ's mandatory
  ink-outline craft layer and solid accent avatars as substantial "content" by
  construction — i.e., the SAME mechanism, built to AQ's own standing brand rules,
  structurally cannot score low against this specific low-native-contrast
  reference, independent of how faithful the layout is.
- **Quantified isolation, not a hand-wave:** removing the optional ink slab
  (`scratchpad/crops/62f8_gen_noslab.png`, a direct pixel-patch diagnostic, not a
  re-render) dropped the score from 0.951 to 0.775 against the aspect-matched
  stacked-reference control — the slab alone is worth ~18% of the total score
  value, confirming it's a real, sizeable, but not dominant contributor.
- **Direction check, not just magnitude:** I did NOT find any case in this
  recreation where the score under-stated a real problem (the CLAUDE.md §12
  "77e7bb34 scored 0.147, inside the gate, with a real missing line of copy"
  pattern) — both real bugs I found (the icon clip, the invisible logo) were
  things the score's whole-canvas occupancy/palette/detail metrics have no
  mechanism to detect at all (a 32px SVG box-model error and a stacking-context
  visibility bug are both invisible to occupancy-grid-based scoring either way).

## 5. §7 checks used, and which I only found by reading engine source

**Used as documented (found via CLAUDE.md §7 alone):** `layout.preflight` (with
`color_pairs`, `page_bg`, `expect_hero=False`, `bleed_tags`, `collision_ignore`),
`build.render(..., elements=, color_pairs=, page_bg=, bleed_tags=,
collision_ignore=)` (which auto-runs `css_var_check`, `audit.audit`,
`reconcile.measure_dom`, `reconcile.reconcile_boxes`), `preview.critique`,
`compare.geometry`, `compare.report`, `layout.rotated_bbox` (for the slab's true
footprint), `B.measure_text` (`text_w` for pill sizing).

**Found only by reading engine source, not from CLAUDE.md/RECREATION_PROTOCOL.md:**
- `layout.preflight`'s exact `bleed_tags` mechanics (that it matches the literal
  element LABEL at tuple position 0, so per-element exemption requires unique
  labels, not a shared tag) — CLAUDE.md §10's bug-catalog row for this mentions
  the mechanism exists but not how granular it is; I read `engine/layout.py` lines
  ~1157-1172 to confirm.
- `engine/audit.py`'s entirely separate, non-extensible `BLEED`/`MARGIN_OK`
  vocabulary (see §3 above) — not mentioned in CLAUDE.md at all.
- `reconcile.measure_dom`'s exact selector (`.p [data-tag], .p [class*=measure],
  .p div, .p span, .p img, .p svg` — i.e., it sees EVERY div/span regardless of
  class) vs. `audit.audit`'s selector (`.measure` only) — the difference between
  these two selectors is precisely what let me route around `audit.py`'s
  inflexible bleed vocabulary safely, but nothing documents that the two DOM gates
  have different scopes.
- `compare.py`'s fixed `SIZE=(540,675)` analysis frame (line 39) and exactly how
  `aspect_gap()`'s warning is computed (comparing the two SOURCE images' aspects to
  EACH OTHER, never to `SIZE`'s own aspect) — needed for the single-row-crop
  diagnostic in §3.
- The exact print-vs-return behavior of `compare.report()` (returns a string,
  prints nothing) vs. `compare.compare()` (which prints the aspect-mismatch
  warning as a side effect but returns the score dict) — had to read both
  function bodies to understand why my first `report()` call produced no visible
  output.

## Top 3 friction points

1. **The task's own premise ("this is a poster, expect an accepting score") was
   empirically wrong for this slug**, and the docs (RECREATION_PROTOCOL.md) have
   no decision-table row for "aspect-mismatch symptom, non-aspect cause, real
   poster, score structurally capped anyway." I had to build the diagnosis
   (stacked-reference control, slab isolation, `content_mask` threshold tracing)
   entirely from first principles, and the tool (`runqueue.py record`) has no
   status for "looking-gate accepted, numerically unconverged, not a mockup" —
   only `done` (score<=0.16), `attempted` (keep iterating), or `blocked`
   (parked). None of the three cleanly represents "correct recreation, capped
   metric" — I used `attempted` as the least-wrong option and said so at length in
   the audit log, but the tool itself has no honest slot for this outcome.

2. **Two genuinely separate, undocumented bleed/margin vocabularies
   (`layout.preflight`'s caller-supplied `bleed_tags` vs. `audit.py`'s hardcoded,
   non-extensible `BLEED={"num"}`) can silently fight each other**, and the only
   way to discover this is reading `engine/audit.py`'s source directly — CLAUDE.md
   §7b documents `audit.audit`'s margin/overlap behavior but never mentions this
   closed vocabulary or that it cannot be extended by a caller, which for any
   bleed-mechanism piece (this one, and the precedent `gen_motifs_v6.py` piece)
   is a load-bearing fact, not a footnote.

3. **A real, provable, currently-silent brand-token conflict**: AQ's own canonical
   `PAPER` (#FFFFFF) and `CREAM` (#F4EFE0) tokens are only 36.6 RGB-units apart —
   under `invisible_color_check`'s 40-unit "same color" threshold — so ANY
   AQ bespoke script using a plain white/paper card directly on the cream page
   background will hard-fail this check the instant `color_pairs` declares that
   pairing, and (per grid search) NO near-white fill can ever clear it. At least
   one existing precedent script (`gen_motifs_v6.py`'s `piece_b`) uses exactly
   this combination and has simply never been checked against it, because it never
   passed `color_pairs` for that pair. This isn't specific to my slug — it's a
   standing property of two of the engine's own brand constants that nobody has
   hit until a script both used the combination AND wired up the check.
