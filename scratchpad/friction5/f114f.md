## Was the MOCKUPS section enough to work from?

**Step 0, "crop before you measure," has zero method attached.** The entire instruction
is one sentence: `compare.crop(ref, x0,y0,x1,y1, out)`, coordinates as canvas fractions.
Nothing says HOW to find x0/y0/x1/y1. What I actually did:

1. Resampled the 640x399 reference 3-4x with `PIL.Image.resize(..., LANCZOS)` and viewed
   it, because at native resolution the bezel-vs-screen edge is 2-3px and invisible.
2. Took raw numpy pixel scans (`a[y, x]` triplets printed to console) along a handful of
   rows/columns to find the near-black bezel band numerically — e.g. at `x=140` the
   notch is a literal black rectangle from y=49 to y=60, distinct from the bezel's own
   black band at y=42-51 and y=61 resuming to the screen. Nothing in either doc mentions
   that a phone mockup has a NOTCH that is a second, separate near-black region nested
   inside the "screen interior" y-range, so a first crop attempt still had a sliver of
   it in frame.
3. Cropped, viewed at 5x, saw residual bezel curve, trimmed a few more px, repeated
   THREE times (`crop_v1` -> `crop_v2` -> `crop_v3`) until nothing was visible at 5x zoom.
4. This whole loop is ~15 minutes of manual scan/crop/view/repeat, done from scratch,
   because there is no tool for it. A `compare.find_bezel(path)` that returns a candidate
   interior box (largest near-uniform light rectangle inside a near-black rounded band)
   would turn this into one call, and every one of the remaining ~20+ mockups in the
   queue would benefit from it — this is not a one-off need.

**A second, subtler problem: even after a "clean-looking" crop, `compare.geometry()`
lies about the content bbox if a corner sliver of bezel curve survives.** My first crop
(`crop_v1`, before the 5x-zoom trim) reported content bbox `x 0.000..0.998, y
0.000..0.999` — i.e. "content fills the entire frame" — purely because a few px of
rounded bezel corner touched all four edges. This would send a builder chasing a
full-bleed margin that does not exist. I only caught it by noticing the bbox was
suspiciously exactly `0..1` on BOTH axes (a real full-bleed design would still have SOME
internal margin variation). There is no check for this — you find it by being
suspicious of your own numbers.

**A rule I can actually propose from this session:** when a crop's `content_bbox` from
`compare.geometry()` comes back within ~0.01 of `0..1` on an axis where you did NOT
deliberately crop to a hard edge, treat it as bezel contamination and re-trim before
trusting anything else the geometry call reports. This would have saved the false lead
on my first crop attempt.

**Canvas choice worked well and is worth promoting from "precedent" to "rule."** The
MOCKUPS section says nothing about WHICH AQ canvas to render onto — I only knew to use
`story` (1080x1920) because I read two prior parked entries in `RECREATION_AUDIT.md`
(`522f2d898b827f`, `110a5730e3710b`) that had independently discovered the same thing.
Computing `aspect_gap` against all of `core.SIZES` and picking the minimum should just be
a step in the protocol, not something three separate sessions have to rediscover by
reading each other's old write-ups. For this reference: crop aspect 0.500 vs story's
0.5625 (11% gap) vs feed's 0.8 (42% gap) vs square's 1.0 (78% gap) — story is obviously
right, and `compare._ASPECT_WARN` (0.25) would not have fired for feed or square either
were you to compute it before choosing a canvas, which is a second point in favour of
just adding "compute aspect_gap against every core.SIZES entry, use the closest" as an
explicit protocol step rather than an author's judgment call.

## The control-run branch the protocol doesn't have

RECREATION_PROTOCOL.md's MOCKUPS section: "run a control... If your control comes back
near zero, the gap is the aspect. If your control comes back large, the gap is real."
My control: **0.159**, against my raw score of 0.554-0.576. That is:
- NOT near zero (unlike `110a5730e3710b`'s 0.001).
- NOT "large" in the sense the two precedents used the word (`522f2d89`'s control was
  1.12 against a measured detail-ratio of 2.49 — i.e. the control was itself close to
  half the total gap, still leaving a large real residual, but at least it was reasoned
  about explicitly).
- It is a THIRD case: a control that is itself comparable in size to the accept
  threshold. This means even a hypothetically PERFECT recreation, scored by this
  pipeline against this specific low-res JPEG crop, could not be expected to score much
  below ~0.16 — the resolution/compression gap between a crisp vector render and a
  640x399-native photo of a phone is baked in. But it also means the 0.554-0.576 I
  measured is NOT fully explained by that artifact — there's a real ~0.4 left over.

The protocol should say what to do here. I chose to park anyway (same category, looking
gate passes, residual real gap too large to call "accepted," and RECREATION_AUDIT.md's
own precedent for a REAL, non-mockup poster with a stubborn residual gap is to `record`
it honestly as `attempted` rather than force either an accept or a park — see `80cb7ed71a8cc9`
and `fe7b3`'s entries). A mockup with a moderate, non-dismissable control sits in between
those two documented paths, and nothing tells you which one wins. I picked "park"
because the CROP ITSELF remains structurally non-comparable (different native
resolution, different compression, different rendering pipeline) even though the
CONTENT gap is partly real — but this is my judgment call, not something the doc gave me.

## Every place the docs were wrong, stale, ambiguous, or missing a step

1. **§6's bespoke-script template has no binder-clip or flame/fire primitive**, and there
   is no guidance for what to do when the reference's stickers are things the vocabulary
   (`doodles.py`'s 20 stamps, `shapes.py`'s 9 parametric silhouettes) simply doesn't cover.
   I hand-wrote both as inline SVG paths. This is the same class of gap `f80cb.md`
   reported for a ribbon/band motif (now fixed with `shapes.ribbon()`) — clips and flames
   are common enough in reference material (emoji-style stickers) that a small
   `shapes.py` addition (`flame()`, maybe a generic `clip()`) seems likely to pay off
   again, though I can't point to a second concrete instance in this session the way
   `f80cb` could.
2. **§9's craft-layer rule ("thick ink outlines, hard-offset shadows") reads as
   mandatory, but the reference's actual mechanism is a SOFT, blurred card shadow** (a
   phone-UI elevation shadow, not AQ's flat hard-offset). CLAUDE.md's own §2 precedence
   ladder ("the mechanism is the part you may not drop") only exists for Workflow C
   (fresh bespoke builds against a drawn style), not explicitly for Workflow B
   recreation — I had to import that reasoning by analogy to justify keeping a soft
   shadow instead of `core.hard_shadow()`'s tiny flat offset. Workflow B's own docs
   (§5, RECREATION_PROTOCOL.md) never mention this tension at all, even though "the
   reference's craft mechanism conflicts with the house craft constant" seems like
   exactly the kind of thing a recreation (not just a fresh Workflow C build) would hit.
3. **§9's Instrument-Serif rule ("<=1 accent word, italic") is a hard brand constant with
   no Workflow-B-specific carve-out, but this reference's ENTIRE body paragraph is set in
   an upright serif.** This is the same shape of conflict as point 2 — a Workflow-B
   reference's own mechanism (serif voice on the whole note) collides with a hard brand
   rule that has no stated Workflow-B exception. I resolved it the same way the §2
   ladder would for Workflow C (keep the RULE, express the mechanism partially — one
   italic accent word) but had to reason my way there; RECREATION_PROTOCOL.md never
   raises this as a possibility even though CLAUDE.md's own catalog of "hard rule beats
   the recipe" language is written for Workflow C specifically.
4. **`compare.geometry()`'s content_mask has no bezel-awareness** (already covered above)
   — worth restating here as a concrete, reproducible bug: run it on a mockup crop with
   even a few px of residual device bezel and it silently reports a bogus "content fills
   the whole frame" bbox, with no warning.
5. **`reconcile_boxes`' matching is purely positional (nearest declared origin, `tol*4`
   = 64px), with no notion of element identity, and this broke on a ROTATED element
   specifically.** My `note_card` was declared at its PRE-rotation origin `(76, 340)`.
   Its actual rotated (-4deg) bounding box, as drawn, starts at `(38, 310)` — a rotated
   large rectangle's top-left corner moves substantially from the unrotated declaration.
   That happened to land within 64px of my UNRELATED `star` element's declared origin
   `(40, 205)`... no, in practice it landed close enough to be picked up as "the star,"
   producing four bogus `BBOX UNDER-REPORTS star: declared 190x190, drawn 977x1190`
   lines — 977x1190 is exactly the rotated note_card's real footprint
   (`900*cos4 + 1130*sin4 ~ 977`, `1130*cos4 + 900*sin4 ~ 1190`), misattributed to a
   190px star. This is a real, reproducible bug class: ANY large rotated element whose
   rotated bbox origin coincides with an unrelated small element's declared origin will
   silently misreport. Given `reconcile_boxes`' own docstring already admits "heavily
   overlapping elements can match the wrong partner," rotation should probably be called
   out explicitly as a second cause, since it moves the ORIGIN, not just the size.
6. **Two independent overlap gates use two different keys, still, in the current
   version.** `[v2.png] IGNORE PAIRS NAME NO data-tag IN THE DOM: [...] this gate matches
   data-tag, while layout.collision_check matches your element LABELS.` fired on every
   render because I never added `data-tag` attributes to my divs (nothing in §6's
   template does either). CLAUDE.md's own bug catalog documents this exact split
   (`audit.audit(ignore_pairs=)` vs `layout.collision_check`, session 10f agent c3) as
   fixed by `render()` forwarding one declaration to both — but the DOM-level gate still
   silently does nothing useful without `data-tag`, and the warning fires unconditionally
   for anyone who doesn't know to add them. The template should either add `data-tag`
   automatically from the label tuple, or the warning should say "add data-tag=label to
   your divs," not just describe the mismatch.
7. **A trivial but real one:** a redundant full-bleed ground `<div>` (matching `page()`'s
   own `.p` background exactly) tripped `invisible_fill` with `distance 0.0` — correct
   behaviour, but worth flagging that the §6 template doesn't warn "don't paint the
   ground twice," and it's an easy first-draft mistake when you're used to Workflow A's
   `bggeo()` pattern of layering things on top of a base.

## API surprises — gates that fired on nothing, or stayed silent on something real

- **Fired correctly, cost time to diagnose:** `OFF-CANVAS dood: box (-17,273)-(247,537)`
  for a rotated star. The fix (push the element in from the edge by the rotated-bbox
  expansion, `size*(|cos|+|sin|)-size`) is exactly what CLAUDE.md's `rotated_bbox` bug-
  catalog row describes, but I had to compute the expansion by hand — `layout.py` HAS
  `rotated_bbox(x,y,w,h,deg)` as a helper but nothing in the template or protocol tells
  you to call it BEFORE placing a rotated doodle near an edge; I only found it by
  reading `layout.py`'s function list after the fact.
- **False positive requiring a documented ignore, not a real bug:** the `note_text`/
  `fire` collision (§ above) — a stale, over-generous declared bbox overlapping an
  unrelated element's bbox in RECTANGLE space while the actual glyphs never come close.
  Fixed by shrinking the declared box to the real measured size (761x665, reported by
  `reconcile_boxes` itself, which is a nice bit of self-correcting feedback once you
  know to read the advisory lines) plus one documented `collision_ignore` entry for the
  residual.
- **Silent on nothing I could find** — the static gate stack (`preflight`, `measure_dom`,
  `reconcile_boxes`) caught every real bug I introduced (the redundant ground div, the
  off-canvas star, the stale text bbox) and only false-positived on things explainable by
  reading its own docstrings. No complaints there.

## Iteration times / what I had to guess

- Crop selection (step 0): ~15-20 minutes of manual pixel-scan-and-view, no shortcut
  available (see above).
- Each render (`PYTHONIOENCODING=utf-8 python scratchpad/gen_114f2b19_vN.py`): a few
  seconds (single render, no `B.session()` batching needed for 4 sequential versions).
- Guessed, not measured: the note card's exact tilt angle (-4deg — eyeballed against the
  reference, not pixel-measured; at this native resolution sub-degree measurement isn't
  meaningful), the flame's exact bezier control points (built by eye to READ as a flame,
  not traced from the reference's actual emoji), and the binder clip's proportions
  (no reference primitive existed to check against, so it's an invented icon in the
  spirit of the source, not a measurement).
- Measured, not guessed (once I bothered to grid it): the note card's true bbox fraction
  (~83% width x ~59% height of the screen — my own FIRST-DRAFT written checklist had
  guessed ~65%x~45% by eye before gridding the crop, and was off by a lot; see the
  audit entry's correction). This is the same "eyeballed proportion becomes a confident
  wrong instruction" trap `compare.geometry()` exists to catch for the OVERALL
  composition — it is not currently caught for INDIVIDUAL elements inside a written
  step-1 inventory, which is exactly where mine was wrong.

## Top 3 friction points

1. **No method for finding the bezel/screen boundary in a mockup crop** — pure manual
   pixel-scanning, ~15-20 min, every time. A `compare.find_bezel()` helper would pay for
   itself immediately given every remaining queue item is a mockup or sheet.
2. **The control-run decision rule has only two branches (near-zero / large) and my
   control landed almost exactly on the accept threshold itself** — a real gap in the
   MOCKUPS section that this session's specific case exposed; the near-precedents
   (522f2d89, 110a5730) didn't hit it because their controls were unambiguous.
3. **`reconcile_boxes`' pure-positional matching breaks specifically on rotated
   elements**, because rotation moves the bounding-box ORIGIN, not just its size — a
   large rotated card's real drawn origin coincidentally lands near an unrelated small
   element's declared origin, and the mismatch report attributes the wrong box to the
   wrong label. Not blocking (advisory-only), but actively confusing to read.
